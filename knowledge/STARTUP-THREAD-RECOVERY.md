# Startup Thread Recovery Register

## Purpose

This file records the material business, service, pricing, operating, technology, and partnership decisions recovered from the Startup project that were previously compressed, omitted, or represented only as short summaries in the repository.

It is a provenance and reconciliation document. Canonical operating rules belong in the detailed files under `docs/`.

## Current personnel rule

Earlier Startup discussions assigned named individuals to several founder functions. Those personal assignments are superseded. The company design now uses role-based functions only.

The repository must not infer current ownership, participation, titles, authority, compensation, or employment from historical named-person discussions.

## Recovered established directions

### Business model

- Founder-led or principal-led technology advisory, implementation, security, automation, and managed-governance company.
- Operating formula: advisory at the front, standardized implementation in the middle, recurring governance at the back.
- Microsoft is the primary specialization and commercial anchor, but the company remains vendor-flexible across customer business systems.
- Initial core customer range is approximately 10–150 employees, with a broader practical range of roughly 1–200 users.
- The company is not primarily a commodity help desk, staffing company, license-markup company, or unrestricted custom-development shop.
- Projects establish a supportable baseline; recurring governance creates durable accountability and revenue.

### Role architecture

The operating model requires the following functions, regardless of who fills them:

1. Commercial leadership and relationships
2. Technology, security, and strategy leadership
3. Delivery and service operations
4. Finance, legal, risk, and administration
5. Partner and ecosystem management

One person may initially perform multiple functions. Assignments and decision rights must be documented before launch.

### Secure Microsoft 365 Foundation

Two product variants were developed:

#### Foundation Core

- Intended primarily for organizations with fewer than 10 users.
- Estimated delivery effort: 26–32 hours.
- Working price range: $6,000–$9,000.
- Historical segmentation: approximately $6,000 for 1–3 users, $7,000–$8,000 for 4–7 users, and approximately $9,000 for 8–10 users.
- Retains Entra identity baseline, MFA, Conditional Access, administrative separation, privileged controls, Intune/device trust, Defender, email protection, basic data authority, incident readiness, and a clean documentation snapshot.
- Defers or reduces deep Secure Score tuning, broad SaaS review, advanced retention scoping, documentation depth, and selected future-state controls.

#### Full Foundation

- Intended for controlled greenfield or cooperative takeover environments, generally 10 or more users.
- Base delivery estimate: 46 hours before complexity adjustments.
- Working price range: $12,000–$18,000.
- Detailed base estimate:
  - Tenant foundation: 2 hours
  - Administrative hygiene: 3 hours
  - Identity baseline: 2 hours
  - Privileged Identity Management / privileged controls: 2 hours
  - Intune foundation: 4 hours
  - Device compliance: 2 hours
  - Endpoint security: 2 hours
  - Security baselines: 2 hours
  - Update rings: 1 hour
  - Email and Defender for Office 365: 2 hours
  - Secure Score review and tuning: 5 hours
  - Data architecture and authority: 2 hours
  - Retention: 3 hours
  - Backup/recovery review: 1 hour
  - SaaS application control: 1 hour
  - Logging and incident readiness: 2 hours
  - Documentation, quality assurance, and customer review: 10 hours
- Historical security outcome target for a suitable environment: Microsoft Secure Score approximately 75–80%, with exceptions documented rather than blindly optimizing the score.

The earlier $3,500–$7,500 Foundation range is retained only as historical context from a less complete estimate. It must not be presented as the current full-service pricing baseline.

### Commercial architecture

The Microsoft-oriented commercial model has three separate layers:

1. One-time Foundation or remediation project
2. Licenses passed through cleanly, generally at Microsoft list price through an indirect-CSP/distributor model
3. Ongoing Microsoft Security and Governance service

Historical recurring hypotheses:

- $500–$2,000+ monthly governance retainers
- $5–$15 per user monthly for lighter governance
- Broader managed bundles at approximately $75–$100, $125–$150, and $175+ per user
- Broader fractional advisory retainers at approximately $2,000–$10,000 monthly

These remain validation ranges, not approved public price lists.

### Partner and distributor direction

- Launch through an indirect CSP/distributor rather than building independent billing, provisioning, credit, support-escalation, and marketplace infrastructure.
- Pax8 is the presumptive launch distributor pathway recovered from the working model.
- Pax8 still requires current commercial, security, support, integration, and contract diligence before execution.
- The architecture should remain portable enough to change distributors later.
- Microsoft partner participation, specialist partners, referral partners, and independent licensed professionals remain structural parts of the model.
- Independent audit, attestation, legal, tax, and other regulated work remains with qualified independent providers.

### Technology and delivery architecture

Recovered technology direction includes:

- Microsoft 365, Entra ID, Intune, Defender, Purview, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive
- Azure Virtual Desktop and Windows 365 where appropriate
- Azure Lighthouse or equivalent least-privileged delegated multi-tenant administration
- PowerShell, Microsoft Graph, Logic Apps, Power Automate, and APIs for repeatable automation
- Sentinel or appropriately scaled logging and security monitoring where justified
- Standardized tenant policies, tags, security baselines, runbooks, evidence, and exception records
- PSA/service-management, CRM, documentation, finance, secret-management, and reporting systems selected against requirements rather than familiarity

Historical product candidates included HaloPSA or Freshservice for service operations and SharePoint/Loop for working documentation. These are candidates, not approved standards.

### Knowledge and solution system

The company should maintain structured relationships among:

- Business use cases
- Solution patterns and packaged services
- Customer projects
- Required skills and contributors
- Partners
- Deliverables and evidence
- Risks, assumptions, and exceptions
- Lessons learned and reusable artifacts

A Dataverse implementation was explored, but repository-first Markdown and structured data remain the launch implementation. A dedicated platform should be introduced only after real operating usage proves the need.

The Top 200 solution-opportunity catalog is a commercial and delivery asset. Opportunities should be ranked by expected business impact relative to time, effort, risk, maintainability, and support burden.

### Launch and financial hypotheses

Recovered early operating hypotheses:

- 18-month agility horizon for the initial model
- Initial setup costs of approximately $5,000–$10,000
- Monthly operating expenses of approximately $1,500–$3,000 before meaningful staffing expansion
- Average recurring client revenue hypothesis of approximately $3,000–$5,000 monthly
- Break-even hypothesis near three recurring clients
- Long-run operating profit target hypothesis of approximately 20–25%

These assumptions require a current financial model and must not be treated as validated forecasts.

### Legal and ownership direction

Earlier discussions favored a single-member LLC at launch, with possible S-corporation tax election after consistent profitability. Additional contributors would initially be contractors or revenue participants, with any equity earned through vesting rather than granted casually at inception.

This remains a historical working direction requiring current legal and tax advice. It is not an executed governance decision.
