# Expanded Startup Project Context

## Purpose

This document consolidates the business decisions, operating assumptions, commercial structures, service concepts, founder functions, partner model, delivery standards, technical direction, and launch sequence developed across the Startup project and clearly related cross-thread refinements through August 18, 2026.

It is intended to prevent Codex and future contributors from reopening settled conceptual work. The repository should use this material as the presumptive operating design unless a founder explicitly changes it through a decision record.

## Business model baseline

[COMPANY_NAME] is a founder-led technology advisory, implementation, security, automation, and managed-governance company.

Its core operating formula is:

> Advisory at the front, standardized implementation in the middle, and recurring governance at the back.

The company gives smaller and mid-sized organizations access to enterprise-quality technology judgment, architecture, security, automation, and operating discipline without requiring them to build a complete internal technology department.

The business is not being designed from scratch. The following are established baseline directions:

- Microsoft is the principal platform specialization.
- The company is not Microsoft-only.
- The company works across the customer’s broader application and vendor environment.
- Strategy and architecture precede implementation.
- Foundation and remediation projects create a supportable baseline.
- Recurring governance and selective managed support create durable revenue.
- Security, documentation, maintainability, and operational ownership are built into delivery.
- Partners, distributors, and independent professional firms are structural components of the operating model.
- Licensing margin is supplemental rather than the primary value proposition.
- The company should productize repeatable work while retaining senior judgment for exceptions and complex decisions.

## Market baseline

The initial core customer is a Microsoft-centric organization with approximately 10–150 employees. The broader practical range is roughly 1–200 users. A deliberate secondary entry segment is 1–9-user owner/operator and early-stage businesses that need to start quickly while remaining secure and compliant; Foundation Core is the scoped offer for that segment.

Typical conditions include:

- No experienced internal CIO, CTO, security leader, or cloud architect
- One internal IT generalist or an incumbent outsourced provider
- Microsoft 365 deployed without consistent governance
- Inconsistent identity, device, collaboration, and data controls
- Manual administrative workflows
- Multiple disconnected SaaS and line-of-business systems
- Weak documentation and unclear ownership
- Increasing insurance, customer, contractual, or compliance requirements
- A desire to modernize without funding a large transformation program

Likely buyers include owners, founders, presidents, COOs, CFOs, IT managers, operations leaders, and security or compliance leaders.

A secondary market exists in larger SMB and enterprise organizations for targeted architecture, automation, integration, security, compliance-readiness, remediation, and virtual-workspace engagements.

## Revenue architecture

The revenue model has four connected layers:

1. Assessment and foundation projects
2. Implementation and remediation projects
3. Recurring advisory, governance, security, automation maintenance, and managed services
4. Licensing and partner-related revenue

Projects create immediate value and establish standards. Recurring services create predictable revenue and continuing accountability.

## Delivery model

The core flow is:

The delivery lifecycle is:

3. Strategy and target architecture
4. Scope, assumptions, exclusions, and agreement

6. Testing and acceptance
7. Documentation and handoff
8. Recurring governance and continuous improvement

The founder functions are baseline operating assumptions. The developed working economic model is a 51% controlling-founder floor, 20% total sales pool, 20% total execution pool, and 9% future-use reserve, with no dedicated advisor pool. The initial cohort is expected to pair one strong sales lead with multiple execution contributors across an overall three-to-five-person group. These are working founder terms—not executed grants. Exact individual allocations, per-deal vesting rates, compensation, voting instruments, legal authority, employment status, and departure terms remain legal and founder-governance decisions.

## Partnership and distribution baseline

Partnerships are part of the business architecture, not an optional future channel.

### Microsoft partnership

The company should pursue the appropriate Microsoft partner pathway for credibility, enablement, customer licensing, managed services, security capabilities, incentives, co-selling, and marketplace opportunities.

### Distributor / indirect CSP pathway

The intended launch model is to use an authorized cloud distributor or indirect CSP rather than constructing independent licensing, billing, provisioning, support, and credit infrastructure.

Distributor capabilities should include:

- Subscription provisioning
- Consolidated billing
- License management
- Technical escalation
- Partner enablement
- Adjacent cloud and security products
- Financing or payment support
- Marketplace access
- Integration and automation options

## Launch sequence

### Phase 1 — Foundation

- Finalize name

- Establish banking and accounting

- Prepare contracts

- Approach the warm network
- Sell assessments or foundation projects
- Deliver pilots
- Refine scope, effort, and pricing
- Capture evidence and case studies
- Convert appropriate customers to recurring services

### Phase 3 — Standardization

- Implement PSA and documentation processes
- Productize recurring services
- Formalize technical templates
- Build the solution catalog
- Improve discovery and proposals
- Separate standard delivery from custom consulting

### Phase 4 — Partner-led growth

- Expand Microsoft partner capabilities
- Formalize distribution
- Develop referral relationships
- Add specialist capacity
- Offer packaged or marketplace solutions where justified
