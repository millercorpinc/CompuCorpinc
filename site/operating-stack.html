Operations

Working playbook

Choose the smallest secure operating stack that supports real work.

The team should establish process and information ownership before choosing products, then use pilot evidence to refine rather than over-buying early.

Selection sequence

Design the work before selecting the tools.

1. Define

  1. Define the minimum process
  2. Define required information
  3. Define security and integration requirements

2. Select

  1. Compare products
  2. Select the smallest viable stack
  3. Configure standards and ownership

3. Prove

  1. Document and test
  2. Reassess after pilot clients

Required capabilities

What the launch stack must support.

Company foundation

  • Identity, email, endpoint management, and security
  • Collaboration, document management, password/secret management
  • Backup, continuity, monitoring, and audit logging

Commercial operations

  • CRM and opportunity tracking
  • Quoting, proposals, e-signature
  • Accounting, invoicing, collections, license, and vendor management

Delivery operations

  • Project and task management
  • Time and cost tracking
  • Service requests, escalation, documentation, knowledge, and automation

Avoid

These shortcuts create operating debt before revenue exists.

Do not overbuild

Do not buy an enterprise stack before processes and ownership are proven.

Do not duplicate

Do not store the same fact in multiple systems without an authoritative owner.

Do not weaken controls

Do not use customer production data for demos, shared admin accounts, undocumented exceptions, or familiarity as the only selection criterion.
