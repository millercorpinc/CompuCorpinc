Foundation team workspace

Launch readiness is a sequence of evidence, not a checklist of intentions.

The first goal is a legally established, commercially ready, secure, and deliverable company capable of earning and completing a paid pilot. Work remains provisional until the stated evidence exists.

Current status: a working founder model now exists—51% controlling-founder floor, 20% sales pool, 20% execution pool, and 9% future-use reserve, with no advisor pool. Exact grants, vesting rates, legal instruments, entity structure, name, approved pricing, partner selection, and first pilot remain open. This portal records the model; it does not execute legal terms.

Five stage gates

What must be true before the company advances.

Gate 1

Founder alignment

Confirm participants, role expectations, decision rights, ownership principles, departure process, capital, and compensation principles.

Evidence

Founder term sheet and accountability matrix ready for counsel.

Gate 2

Legal readiness

Approve the name, form the entity, establish tax and banking, obtain core insurance, and prepare the contract framework.

Evidence

Formation records, operating accounts, insurance review, and counsel-reviewed contract package.

Gate 3

Commercial readiness

Choose the initial customer profile and offer. Define scope, exclusions, pricing, discovery, proposal materials, and partner dependencies.

Evidence

Approved service brief, cost model, sales kit, and proposal process.

Gate 4

Delivery readiness

Run the company to its own security standard and establish access, delivery, acceptance, escalation, documentation, and capacity processes.

Evidence

Tested internal baseline, delivery playbook, runbooks, and acceptance templates.

Gate 5

Paid pilot

Convert a qualified opportunity, collect according to approved terms, deliver the standard process, and capture the evidence.

Evidence

Signed SOW, customer acceptance, closeout, lessons, and recurring-governance proposal.

90-day focus

Prepare, validate, then deliver.

Days 1–30

  • Founder and legal decisions
  • Finance, insurance, contracts
  • Initial customer and offer
  • Internal identity, endpoints, and secrets

Days 31–60

  • Warm-network account list
  • Discovery and proposal package
  • Delivery and handoff process
  • Microsoft and distributor research

Days 61–90

  • Contract the first paid pilot
  • Measure planned vs. actual effort
  • Capture proof and feedback
  • Offer recurring governance
