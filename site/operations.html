Operating system

One disciplined path from qualified conversation to governed customer outcome.

The business is not organized around selling tools or providing unlimited support. It leads with senior judgment, delivers repeatable standards, and creates recurring accountability after the work is accepted.

Launch rule: no customer work begins without appropriate scope, payment terms, named stakeholders, secure access, and an accountable delivery owner.

Sales to governance

How the company earns trust, proves value, and continues ownership.

1. Target and discover

Focus on organizations with a real technology condition, accountable buyer, urgency, feasible environment, and a plausible budget and timing path.

2. Qualify and shape

Identify the business outcome, risk, current systems, decision process, and customer responsibilities. Use paid assessment work when uncertainty is too high for fixed scope.

3. Propose and contract

State outcome, scope, exclusions, assumptions, acceptance, price, payment, change process, and recurring implications before onboarding.

4. Implement and test

Plan milestones and access, document the target state, pilot high-risk changes, record changes, test normal and exception paths, and manage changes explicitly.

5. Accept and hand off

Deliver architecture and runbooks, obtain acceptance, record open risks, transfer operational ownership, and define support and escalation.

6. Govern and improve

Use operating reviews, configuration maintenance, security review, documentation, roadmap management, and evidence to create durable recurring value.

Delivery controls

What makes delivery professional and repeatable.

At intake

  • Signed scope and payment terms
  • Named sponsor, contacts, and decision-makers
  • Recorded risks, assumptions, dependencies, and responsibilities
  • Secure access and a controlled project workspace

During work

  • Named administrative accounts and least privilege
  • Approved design and exception handling
  • Change records, rollback consideration, and customer communication
  • Documented testing and defect remediation

At closeout

  • Customer acceptance and operational handoff
  • Architecture, runbooks, open risks, and escalation route
  • Planned-versus-actual effort and margin review
  • Reusable lessons, proof, and next governance proposal

Internal baseline

Operate the company to the standard it recommends.

Identity and endpoints

  • Named accounts, MFA, separate privileged access, least privilege
  • Managed, encrypted, patched endpoints with endpoint protection
  • Documented break-glass access and periodic review

Data and secrets

  • Customer separation and controlled external sharing
  • Approved storage, retention, and data classification
  • Secret manager, no shared credentials, rotation, and API-key inventory

Response and continuity

  • Administrative logging, alerting, incident response, evidence preservation
  • Partner access expiration and risk-proportionate reviews
  • Backups, recovery procedures, alternate communications, and tests

Minimum operating stack

Select the smallest secure stack that supports the work.

Core capabilities

  • Identity, endpoints, collaboration, documents, and secrets
  • CRM, quoting, proposals, e-signature, and finance
  • Projects, time and cost tracking, service requests, documentation

Control principles

  • Define process and authoritative information before choosing products
  • Avoid duplicate systems of record and shared admin accounts
  • Do not use customer production data in demos

Reassess after pilots

Tool selection remains open. Configure standards and ownership, document and test the selected stack, then revise from real pilot evidence instead of buying an enterprise suite prematurely.
