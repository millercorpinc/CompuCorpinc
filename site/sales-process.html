Operations

Working playbook

Sell a clear operating improvement, not a generic promise of transformation.

The sales process helps the team qualify the right customer conditions, make an explicit commercial recommendation, and carry delivery evidence into recurring governance.

Eight stages

A disciplined route from target account to account expansion.

1. Target

Identify organizations with a likely trigger, accountable buyer, and a credible fit for the company’s service model.

2. Connect

Use trusted relationships, referrals, focused outreach, or qualified partner introductions.

3. Discover

  • Business condition and desired outcome
  • Current systems and ownership
  • Risk, urgency, internal capabilities, decision process, timeline, and budget context

4. Qualify

  • Real business problem and responsible sponsor
  • Feasible environment and defined next decision
  • Plausible budget and timing with acceptable legal and security risk

5. Shape

Develop the recommended assessment, service, implementation, or advisory path around the customer’s actual condition.

6. Propose

  • Outcome, scope, exclusions, assumptions, responsibilities, and deliverables
  • Acceptance, price, payment, change process, and recurring implications

7. Contract and onboard

Confirm access, stakeholders, payment, schedule, and risk controls before work begins.

8. Expand

Use delivery evidence and operating reviews to identify recurring governance and additional improvement opportunities.

Disqualify early

Walk away from unmanageable delivery risk.

Commercial risk

  • No accountable sponsor
  • Unlimited-support expectation without adequate price
  • Unclear or unacceptable payment risk

Delivery risk

  • Unsupported legacy environment without funded discovery
  • Refusal to meet security prerequisites
  • No feasible operating ownership model

Professional boundary

  • Request for work the company is not qualified to perform
  • Customer demands that create unethical, illegal, or unmanageable risk
