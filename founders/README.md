## Public-disclosure rules

The `founders/` folder is intentionally sanitized for a public GitHub repository.

Do **not** add:

- Client, customer, prospect, partner-contact, coworker, executive, auditor, or recommender names without explicit approval.
- Personal email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, IDs, account numbers, tenant identifiers, IP addresses, hostnames, credentials, secrets, or access details.
- Client-specific project names, incidents, environments, commercial terms, contract details, or identifiable customer facts.
- Internal security weaknesses, active remediation gaps, incident-forensics detail, law-enforcement detail, or implementation information that would materially improve reconnaissance of an employer or client environment.
- Exact internal vendor pricing, SKU-level license counts, contract renewal dates, confidential budget baselines, or other commercially sensitive line-item data.
- Raw internal source documents, emails, audit evidence, spreadsheets, screenshots, or transition trackers.

Public résumé-level metrics may be retained only when they are useful professional outcomes and do not reveal the underlying confidential source data. Prefer rounded language such as `170+ employees`, `60+ systems`, `seven-figure software portfolio`, `>$250K annual reduction`, or `>80% reduction` over detailed internal tables.

## Claim-integrity rules

- Preserve distinctions between **OWNED**, **LED**, **SPONSORED**, **CONTRIBUTED**, **INHERITED**, and **APPROVED** work.
- Do not convert modeled or recommended savings into realized savings.
- Do not claim project ownership where the source record only establishes environment context or participation.
- Prefer quantified, evidence-backed outcomes over generic responsibility statements.
- Reconcile conflicting dates, titles, counts, or metrics explicitly instead of silently choosing one.
- Generate public bios, résumés, and capability statements from this sanitized layer rather than importing private source records directly.
