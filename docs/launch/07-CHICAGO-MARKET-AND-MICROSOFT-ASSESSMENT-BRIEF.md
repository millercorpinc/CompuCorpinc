## Launch market

### Geography

Initial sales and delivery focus: Chicago and Chicagoland, using the founders' warm professional network and local relationships first.

### Prospect tracks

Test four tracks in parallel. The working test is approximately five accounts per track, or about 20 accounts total, but the list may flex based on actual warm relationships and trigger events.

1. Physicians, medical practices, and outpatient clinics
2. CPA, accounting, tax, bookkeeping, and advisory firms
3. Construction, contractors, trades, property, facilities, and other field-service businesses
4. Law firms and other regulated professional-service organizations

The tracks are a launch experiment, not a permanent vertical decision. Compare response, urgency, buying access, delivery complexity, price acceptance, and referral potential.

### Customer fit

Prioritize organizations that:

- have approximately 10-150 employees or users, with smaller and larger exceptions based on fit;
- use Microsoft 365 or intend to standardize on it;
- lack experienced senior technology or security leadership;
- have inconsistent identity, endpoint, collaboration, data, or application controls;
- face growth, acquisition, provider-change, cyber-insurance, customer-security, or compliance pressure;
- want modernization without a large transformation program; and
- have an accountable executive sponsor and practical access to the environment.

## Entry offer

### Offer name

Technology and Security Baseline Assessment, with an optional Microsoft Secure Workplace Foundation implementation.

### Commercial posture

Lead with a paid, fixed-fee assessment. Preferred payment position is 100% upfront, subject to approved exceptions. Pricing remains open until the cost and capacity workbook is completed and reviewed.

### Assessment structure

The default assessment is a one-week Microsoft baseline. Business-systems and vertical-risk work are optional one-week modules when the customer needs them. Do not promise a full business or compliance assessment inside the core week without explicit scope.

#### Core Microsoft baseline week

- Entra ID identity and administrator review
- MFA and Conditional Access
- Privileged access and PIM review where licensed and justified
- Intune enrollment, device management, and compliance baseline
- Defender and email-security posture
- Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, and external-sharing controls
- Licensing and prerequisite review
- Secure Score and prioritized risk review
- Current-state ownership, documentation, and operational handoff needs

#### Optional one-week modules

- Business systems and workflow assessment: applications, manual processes, data flows, integrations, ownership, and automation opportunities
- Vertical-risk and readiness assessment: customer, insurer, privacy, continuity, and industry-specific operating requirements
- Additional migration or takeover discovery where a Foundation or migration scope may follow

Advanced services such as Purview, retention, advanced Defender, and PIM are licensing-dependent and should be validated during discovery. They are not assumed to be available or included in every customer environment.

## Assessment deliverables

Every core assessment should produce:

1. Executive findings report: business impact, priority risks, and recommended decisions
2. Technical baseline report: evidence, configuration findings, dependencies, and remediation backlog
3. Target-state roadmap: phased implementation, migration decisions, ownership, sequencing, and recurring-governance recommendation

Optional modules add their own findings and roadmap sections.

## Microsoft implementation path

A migration or Foundation implementation requires an assessment first unless the controlling founder approves a documented exception. Migration is a separate workstream, not an assumed part of the baseline price.

### Foundation delivery stages

1. Discovery and inventory
2. Baseline assessment
3. Target configuration and exception approval
4. Pilot high-risk changes
5. Production rollout
6. Verification and defect remediation
7. Documentation and operational handoff
8. Recurring governance proposal

### Standard Foundation scope

- Tenant and licensing review
- Administrator hygiene and break-glass controls
- Identity, MFA, Conditional Access, and privileged-access standards
- Intune and device-management baseline
- Device compliance, endpoint hardening, Defender, and update policies
- Email-security baseline
- Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, retention, and sharing foundations where licensed
- SaaS application access review
- Logging and incident-readiness baseline
- Documentation, QA, customer acceptance, and handoff

### Acceptance evidence

- Approved configuration baseline
- Named administrative ownership
- MFA and access-policy evidence
- Managed-device inventory
- Endpoint-security status
- Sharing and collaboration settings
- User-lifecycle runbook
- Open-risk register
- Customer acceptance

## Exclusions and professional boundaries

Unless separately scoped, exclude:

- 24x7 help desk or unlimited end-user support
- Complex tenant-to-tenant migration or large data migration
- Hardware replacement or network redesign
- Unsupported operating systems or applications
- Formal compliance certification, audit, or attestation
- Legal, tax, accounting, or regulatory advice
- Unfunded remediation of every customer application or legacy issue

The company prepares customers to meet requirements through technology controls, evidence, and remediation planning. Qualified independent professionals provide legal advice and formal attestations.
