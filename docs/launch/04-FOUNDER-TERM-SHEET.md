- **Purpose:** summarize the proposed founder bargain before counsel converts approved business terms into formation, ownership, governance, and incentive documents
- **Scope:** true founders and the overall incentive-reserve architecture; contributor awards are governed separately

## Summary

This term sheet records the working founder economics and governance objectives. It deliberately separates:

1. **true founder ownership and founder vesting**; and
2. **sales/execution performance incentives**.

The founder agreement should not force true founder ownership to vest only when customer deals close. Deal-based measurement is primarily an incentive-compensation concept.

## Working assumptions

- The founder carrying the primary legal, financial, and operating responsibility should preserve the intended control outcome.
- No founder receives equal ownership merely because the founders are friends or joined at the same time.
- Founder ownership should reflect long-term commitment, risk, capital, pre-formation contribution, role, and future service.
- Sales and execution are distinct value-creation functions and may use distinct incentive formulas.
- Performance incentives should require objective outcomes and formal plan administration.
- A future-use reserve should remain available for hires, strategic contributors, or future financing.

## Proposed entity architecture

Subject to founder approval and counsel/CPA validation, `ADR-0005` proposes:

- Illinois manager-managed LLC;
- initial federal partnership tax classification;
- founder control encoded through the Operating Agreement, manager appointment/removal, voting rights, and reserved matters;
- separate founder ownership and contributor incentive instruments;
- Delaware deferred absent a concrete financing/investor reason;
- Michigan registration/payroll/tax reviewed as actual Michigan activity develops.

This entity/tax decision remains **proposed**, not approved.

## Founder governance objective

The controlling founder should retain decisive ordinary-course authority consistent with the final Operating Agreement.

The Operating Agreement should define:

- manager appointment/removal;
- ordinary-course management authority;
- bank/signing authority;
- contract/spending thresholds;
- hiring and compensation authority;
- debt/guarantees;
- issuance/admission of new owners;
- reserved member decisions;
- amendments;
- related-party transactions/conflicts;
- sale/merger/dissolution;
- tax elections;
- distributions.

A 51% ownership percentage should not be treated as the sole legal mechanism of control.

## True founder ownership

### Founder / controlling owner

Working expectations:

- carries significant legal, financial, strategic, and operating risk;
- leads company strategy and risk posture;
- holds the manager/control rights established in the Operating Agreement;
- participates in material founder decisions under the agreed reserved-matter structure.

### Other true founders

A person should receive true founder units only when the founders intentionally want that person to become a long-term owner of current enterprise value and participate in the founder bargain.

For each founder, approve:

- ownership range;
- capital/pre-formation contribution;
- expected weekly/service commitment;
- role/accountability;
- voting rights;
- founder vesting/repurchase;
- compensation;
- IP/confidentiality;
- transfer and departure treatment.

## Founder vesting

Counsel should model founder reverse vesting/company repurchase protection.

A common technology-startup benchmark is:

- four-year vesting;
- one-year cliff;
- monthly vesting afterward;
- possible negotiated credit for substantial pre-formation work.

The founders may choose a different schedule. The purpose is to prevent a founder from retaining a disproportionate permanent stake after leaving early.

The final agreement should address:

- voluntary departure;
- termination for cause;
- death/disability;
- change of control;
- repurchase price/mechanics;
- acceleration, if any.

## Sales incentive reserve

The working sales reserve remains 20%, but it is not necessarily a pool of ordinary membership units.

### Founding sales lead

Working commercial concept:

- responsible for revenue strategy, prospecting, relationships, opportunity ownership, and commercial feedback;
- compensated primarily through cash commission/revenue-linked compensation at launch;
- may qualify for a long-term incentive award if the founders intentionally approve it;
- any actual security grant requires the approved plan and securities/tax process.

Independent sales contractors require special securities-law review; Rule 701 should not be assumed to cover them automatically.

## Execution incentive reserve

The working execution reserve remains 20%, but actual implementation may use profits-interest/incentive units, phantom equity, cash profit sharing, or a combination.

Execution incentives should remain tied to objective value creation such as:

- collected revenue;
- contribution margin;
- accepted delivery;
- documented project ownership;
- quality/rework outcomes where appropriate.

See `03-EXECUTION-EQUITY-TERMS.md`, now maintained as a non-executable incentive-design specification.

## Incentive guardrails

- No ownership is created by pipeline, verbal promises, enthusiasm, or an internal spreadsheet alone.
- Performance credits are not securities unless/ until converted through an approved legal award.
- Each contributor has an approved cap/award limit.
- Attribution follows written formulas and contemporaneous company records.
- Actual awards require authorized approval, tax/securities compliance, signed documents, and ledger entry.
- Short-term/intermittent contributors should not become permanent minority owners by default.

## Founder departure and buyout terms

Counsel must address:

- unvested founder units;
- vested founder units;
- company/member purchase rights;
- valuation methodology;
- payment terms;
- good/bad leaver rules if used;
- death/disability;
- divorce;
- bankruptcy/charging orders;
- unauthorized transfer;
- confidentiality/IP/customer obligations;
- pending compensation/distributions.

## Intellectual property

The final founder package should include:

- present assignment of company-created IP;
- schedule of pre-existing/background IP;
- license rights needed for any background IP incorporated into company work;
- prior-employer/conflict review;
- confidentiality/trade-secret obligations;
- federal DTSA notice;
- Illinois invention-assignment requirements where applicable.

## Tax and securities

Before ownership is issued:

- identify capital versus profits interest treatment;
- establish valuation/basis;
- complete Section 83/83(b) workflow where applicable;
- identify federal and state securities exemptions;
- document manager/member approvals;
- update the permanent ownership ledger;
- establish tax distributions if partnership-taxed.

## Open founder decisions

Before counsel can finalize documents, founders should approve or narrow:

1. Which people are **true founders/initial owners**?
2. Is the controlling-founder 51% target still the intended fully diluted and/or voting target?
3. What founder vesting schedule applies to each founder?
4. What pre-formation contribution credit is appropriate?
5. How much cash/capital will each founder contribute?
6. Which decisions are reserved from manager authority?
7. How should death, disability, voluntary departure, and termination affect vested interests?
8. Should long-term contributor incentives use profits interests, phantom equity, or both?
9. How many people do the founders actually want to become legal owners versus economic incentive participants?
10. Is outside equity financing reasonably expected within the next 24–36 months?
