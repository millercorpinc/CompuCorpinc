# Partner Evaluation Checklist

## Purpose

Use this checklist to evaluate partner opportunities before a relationship is approved for client work or licensing operations.

- Partner name:
- Partner category:
- Contact(s):
- Website / profile:
- Date reviewed:
- Reviewed by:
- Decision status: evaluate / shortlist / approved / rejected

## Capability review

- Does the partner provide the required product or service capability?
- Is the capability mission-critical or only supplemental?
- Is the capability available in the required geography or region?
- Are there current examples of similar customer work?
- Is there an internal escalation or technical support model?

## Business review

- Do we understand the economics and margin structure?
- Are there payment terms, credit requirements, or minimum commitments?
- Does the partner operate with a model that is consistent with our business goals?
- Does this relationship create customer confusion or assignment ambiguity?
- Does this add strategic leverage without excessive dependence?

## Customer ownership and governance

- Who owns the customer relationship?
- Who is the contracting party for the engagement?
- Who leads the customer communication and escalation?
- Who owns service quality and issue resolution?
- How are support and escalation responsibilities defined?
- What happens if the partner underperforms or exits?

## Security and risk

- Does the partner meet our security requirements?
- Does the partner handle customer data or configuration data?
- Are confidentiality and data-handling terms in place?
- Are cyber insurance or other coverage requirements satisfied?
- Are there independence concerns or relevant conflicts of interest?

## Legal and commercial review

- Does the MSA or partner agreement align with our operating model?
- Are referral economics and customer ownership clear?
- Are intellectual-property ownership and confidentiality terms clear?
- Are there termination rights and transition obligations?
- Are there audit or attestation obligations that affect our work?

## Operational fit

- Can the partner integrate with our delivery process?
- Is there a clear handoff model?
- Do we have a customer support path and incident process?
- Do we have a documented escalation and communication process?
- Can the partner adapt to our service standards and customer experience goals?

- Recommendation:

## Example: distributor / CSP decision questions

- Does the partner support the required Microsoft licensing model?
- Does it simplify provisioning or billing for our customers?
- Does it provide support and technical escalation when needed?
- Does the partner align with our customer experience and governance standards?
- Does it create customer confusion about who owns the account relationship?
- Is the margin worth the operational complexity?
- Are credit or minimum spend requirements acceptable?
- Are there any vendor lock-in or migration issues?

## Example: specialist decision questions

- Is the specialist truly independent and qualified?
- Will they add value without creating governance confusion?
- Are they necessary to the customer outcome or just optional?
- Do they trigger conflict-of-interest concerns?
- Can their output be reviewed and integrated by our team?

## Closing rule

A partner relationship should never be approved merely because it is available. It should be approved because it clearly supports the company’s accountable customer model, reduces operational risk, and fits within the company’s actual business design.
