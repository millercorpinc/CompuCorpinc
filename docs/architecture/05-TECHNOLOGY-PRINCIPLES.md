# Technology Principles

## Platform posture

Microsoft is the principal platform specialization. Other software is described by capability category unless a specific vendor decision materially affects design.

## Selection principles

- Prefer capabilities the customer already owns when they satisfy requirements.
- Avoid unnecessary platform proliferation.
- Use native integrations when supportable and sufficient.
- Use APIs and standard protocols before fragile UI automation.
- Use desktop automation only when better interfaces are unavailable and controls are adequate.
- Keep custom software small, observable, documented, and supportable.
- Do not automate a broken or undefined process without addressing the process.
- Preserve human approval for high-impact actions.
- Design for customer ownership and transition.
- Record exceptions and technical debt.

## Internal stack requirements

The launch stack must support:

- Identity and access
- Endpoint security
- Collaboration and document management
- CRM and pipeline
- Quoting and contracting
- Project and service management
- Documentation and knowledge
- Accounting and billing
- Password and secret management
- Automation
- Monitoring and logging
- Backup and continuity

Products are not approved until requirements, cost, security, integration, and operational ownership are reviewed.
