## Founder fact sheet to complete

For each proposed founder/owner:

- legal name;
- state of residence;
- citizenship/tax residency if relevant;
- intended role;
- expected weekly capacity;
- employee/contractor/member expectation;
- cash/property contributed;
- pre-formation work contributed;
- expected compensation during first 12 months;
- desired ownership range;
- desired voting/control role;
- outside employment and invention/IP restrictions;
- existing businesses/conflicts;
- expected customer relationships contributed;
- whether spouse/trust ownership is contemplated;
- whether the person expects to remain long-term.

## Business assumptions to give CPA/tax counsel

- expected first-year revenue range;
- expected first-year profit/loss;
- gross-margin target;
- founder cash compensation expectations;
- distributions expected versus retained earnings;
- owner residence by state;
- expected Michigan employee/contractor activity;
- whether company will resell cloud/software/licenses;
- anticipated number of actual equity owners;
- anticipated number of incentive participants;
- whether outside capital is expected in 24–36 months;
- whether conversion to a corporation is plausible.

## Questions counsel must answer in writing

### Entity and jurisdiction

- Is an Illinois manager-managed LLC the recommended formation vehicle?
- Is there any concrete reason to choose Delaware or Michigan instead?
- What exact Articles language should establish manager-managed status?
- What acts require member consent under Illinois law notwithstanding the intended manager authority?
- Should the company file a Statement of Authority?

### Control

- How should the controlling founder's control be encoded?
- Should there be one manager, multiple managers, or a manager board?
- Who appoints/removes managers?
- What decisions should be reserved to members?
- Can economic and voting classes be separated as proposed?
- How will dilution affect control?

### Founder equity

- Who should receive actual founder units at formation?
- Should founder units be capital interests or another form?
- What vesting/repurchase schedule is appropriate?
- How should pre-formation work be credited?
- What 83(b) filings are required or recommended?
- What is the tax basis/valuation at formation?

### Contributor incentives

Ask counsel to present side-by-side documents/economics for:

1. profits-interest / incentive-unit plan; and
2. phantom-equity / unit-appreciation plan.

For each, explain:

- recipient tax treatment;
- company tax treatment;
- W-2/1099/K-1 consequences;
- securities exemption;
- voting/member rights;
- information rights;
- vesting and forfeiture;
- treatment on departure;
- acquisition/change-of-control treatment;
- Section 409A issues if applicable;
- administrative cost;
- future S-election compatibility;
- future C-corporation conversion consequences.

### Sales incentives

- Can the intended sales recipient legally use Rule 701 based on the actual relationship?
- If not, which exemption would support an equity grant?
- Is commission/cash incentive preferable until the person becomes an employee or qualifying service provider?
- How should commission clawbacks/refunds/bad debt be treated?

### Operating Agreement

Require specific treatment of:

- management;
- voting;
- reserved matters;
- fiduciary duties to the extent modifiable;
- conflicts/related-party transactions;
- books and records;
- tax matters and partnership representative;
- allocations;
- distributions/tax distributions;
- capital accounts;
- new issuances/admission of members;
- transfers;
- ROFR/call rights;
- dissociation;
- death/disability;
- divorce/bankruptcy/charging orders;
- expulsion/bad actor;
- valuation/buyout;
- merger/sale/change of control;
- dissolution;
- dispute resolution.

### Securities

- What federal exemption covers each founder issuance?
- What exemption covers each profits-interest/incentive grant?
- What Illinois notice/exemption applies?
- What Michigan notice/exemption applies for Michigan recipients?
- When is Form D required?
- What disclosure package should be retained?
- Who is responsible for maintaining the issuance record?

### IP and confidentiality

- present assignment language;
- employee invention statutory notice;
- DTSA whistleblower immunity notice;
- contractor work-made-for-hire + assignment structure;
- pre-existing/background IP schedule;
- open-source disclosure rules;
- founder prior-employer IP/conflict review;
- customer deliverable ownership model.

### Workforce

- classification of each founder/contributor;
- Illinois freelance contract requirements;
- Michigan payroll/foreign-qualification triggers;
- workers-compensation requirements;
- employee handbook/policies needed at first hire;
- restrictive covenant limits by worker state.

### Customer contracts

Request a modular package:

- MSA;
- SOW;
- change order;
- managed-services schedule;
- SLA/support schedule if applicable;
- security/DPA;
- administrative-access authorization;
- software/CSP/reseller schedule;
- BAA form/process;
- subcontractor agreement;
- referral agreement;
- offboarding/transition form.

### Tax / reseller

Ask tax advisor for a separate short memo on:

- Chicago Personal Property Lease Transaction Tax;
- Illinois lease/software tax;
- cloud/software resale and sourcing;
- distributor versus company collection responsibility;
- resale/re-lease documentation;
- invoice line-item design;
- services bundled with cloud subscriptions;
- customer exemptions.

Do not consider formation complete until there is a permanent closing set containing, as applicable:

- approved name clearance evidence;
- Articles of Organization;
- filed/stamped formation evidence;
- EIN confirmation;
- Operating Agreement;
- organizer consent;

- registered-agent information;

- accounting/tax setup;

- insurance binders/policies;

## Before first contractor performs work

Confirm:

- classification memo/check;
- signed contractor agreement and SOW;
- Illinois Freelance Worker Protection Act terms if applicable;
- W-9;
- IP/confidentiality/DTSA terms;
- security/access requirements;
- insurance evidence where required;
- customer flow-downs;
- no actual equity promise outside approved plan.

## Before first employee starts

Confirm:

- payroll registrations;
- workers compensation;
- unemployment registration;
- offer/employment documents;
- I-9 process;
- tax withholding forms;
- IP/invention assignment with required Illinois notice if applicable;
- DTSA notice;
- security/acceptable-use policies;
- restrictive covenant compliance;
- benefits/leave notices required at then-current headcount/location.

## Before first paid customer signs

Confirm:

- legal entity exists and is in good standing;
- city/state business licenses are resolved;
- insurance is active;
- MSA/SOW package is approved;
- signing authority is documented;
- payment/bank process exists;
- security baseline is live;
- privileged-access process is live;
- incident-response owner/process exists;
- customer data type is classified;
- sector module (BAA/GLBA/etc.) triggered if applicable;
- reseller/cloud tax treatment is known if licenses are being sold;
- subcontractor flow-downs are signed;
- acceptance and offboarding are defined.

## Before first actual equity/incentive-unit grant

Confirm:

- Operating Agreement supports the class/instrument;
- plan approved;
- manager/member approval obtained;
- recipient is eligible;
- relationship/classification reviewed;
- valuation/hurdle documented;
- federal securities exemption identified;
- state exemption/notice identified;
- award/joinder signed;
- tax warnings and 83(b) workflow delivered where applicable;
- cap table/unit ledger updated;
- CPA notified;
- permanent grant file stored.

## Counsel red flags to ask about explicitly

- accidental general partnership before LLC formation;
- promises of equity made before plan/entity approval;
- prior-employer IP/confidentiality restrictions on founders;
- founder moonlighting/conflict policies;
- referral fees in regulated industries;
- resale taxes and vendor contract restrictions;
- broad guarantees of compliance/security;
- subcontractors with customer admin access;
- insurance exclusions inconsistent with offered services;
- hiring people in states where company has not registered payroll/foreign qualification;
- customer contracts that require impossible incident-reporting or uncapped liability;
- S-election accidentally invalidated by economic rights.

## Definition of done

The legal workstream is complete enough for launch when the founders can answer:

1. **Who owns what?**
2. **Who controls what?**
3. **How does someone earn more?**
4. **What happens when someone leaves?**
5. **Who owns the IP?**
6. **How are owners/workers taxed and paid?**
7. **Which securities exemption supports each issuance?**
8. **Where is the company registered/licensed?**
9. **What contract governs every customer/partner/contractor relationship?**
10. **What happens when customer data or systems are compromised?**
11. **What taxes apply to software/cloud resale?**
12. **What evidence proves all of the above?**
