# Entity, Jurisdiction, and Tax Analysis

## Source and status

- **Status:** proposed legal/tax architecture; not approved
- **Research date:** 2026-08-19
- **Professional review required:** Illinois business/startup counsel and CPA/tax counsel before formation or equity issuance
- **Assumed operating facts:** founder-led technology advisory/services company; Chicago-first market; at least one to two core participants expected in Chicago and other participants in Michigan; no currently stated institutional-venture financing requirement

## Recommendation

The current best-fit launch structure is:

> **Illinois manager-managed LLC, initially using the federal partnership tax classification, with a deliberately drafted operating agreement and a separate incentive-compensation plan.**

This should remain a proposed decision until counsel and tax review the actual founder roster, residence, expected income, compensation, capital contributions, and incentive design.

## Why an LLC fits this business model

The business expects to combine founder ownership, consulting/service income, recurring services, contractors/specialists, potential profit-sharing, and potentially performance-based incentive ownership. A partnership-taxed LLC can accommodate flexible economics more naturally than an S corporation because an S corporation must maintain one class of stock for federal tax eligibility, meaning its outstanding equity must generally confer identical distribution and liquidation rights even though voting rights may differ.

That flexibility does not make an LLC automatically simpler. A partnership-taxed LLC has its own complexity: K-1s, self-employment treatment for partners performing services, tax allocations, capital accounts, tax distributions, guaranteed payments, and potentially complicated profits-interest accounting.

The decision should therefore be driven by the actual founder/incentive economics rather than the generic statement that “LLCs are simpler.”

## Illinois management architecture

### Default rule to avoid

Illinois law makes an LLC member-managed unless the operating agreement expressly provides for manager management. In a member-managed company, each member has equal rights in management by default.

For this project, that is inconsistent with the intended founder-control model.

### Proposed rule

The operating agreement should expressly establish a **manager-managed** company.

At launch, counsel should evaluate whether the cleanest structure is:

- one initial manager with broad ordinary-course authority; or
- a small board of managers with the controlling founder holding appointment/control rights.

### The 51% issue

The current `51% founder-controlled equity` target may still be useful as an economic and voting design principle, but it is not enough by itself. Illinois law provides default manager appointment/removal rules and unanimous-member rules for certain actions; the operating agreement must be drafted around those statutory provisions rather than assuming that 51% solves every governance question.

Counsel should specifically map:

- appointment and removal of managers;
- manager vacancies;
- ordinary-course decisions;
- annual budget and compensation approvals;
- debt and guarantees;
- major contracts;
- equity/unit issuance;
- admission of new members;
- amendments to the operating agreement;
- amendments to Articles;
- mergers, conversions, domestications, or sale of substantially all assets;
- dissolution;
- related-party transactions;
- litigation settlements above a threshold;
- banking authority;
- tax elections;
- distributions;
- transfers and buybacks.

## Illinois statutory limits on founder discretion

The operating agreement can modify many statutory defaults, but Illinois does not allow everything to be waived away.

The operating agreement cannot, among other statutory limits, unreasonably restrict certain information rights or eliminate the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing. Illinois also permits a member or transferee to seek judicial relief when controlling persons act illegally, fraudulently, or oppressively in a manner directly harmful to that person; the court can order remedies including a buyout rather than dissolution.

Therefore the present draft language stating that the company has “sole and final authority” over attribution and that decisions are “not subject to appeal” should be replaced by a better system:

- defined formulas and source data;
- good-faith determination standard;
- written attribution record;
- correction process for calculation errors;
- conflict procedure when the deciding founder is personally interested;
- optional accountant determination for financial calculations;
- explicit statement that statutory/nonwaivable rights are preserved.

This is more defensible and more operationally trustworthy.

## Tax classification options

### Option A — partnership taxation at launch

This is the current recommendation if the company adopts LLC profits interests or differentiated economics.

Benefits:

- flexible allocations if properly drafted and tax-compliant;
- profits-interest incentive design is available;
- pass-through taxation;
- natural fit for a small number of active owners.

Costs/risks:

- partners performing services are generally self-employed for federal tax purposes, not W-2 employees of the partnership;
- K-1 administration;
- guaranteed-payment and self-employment-tax issues;
- capital-account and allocation complexity;
- tax can be owed on allocated income even when cash is not distributed, requiring a tax-distribution policy;
- adding many small owners creates administrative drag.

### Option B — S-corporation election later

An LLC may potentially elect S-corporation tax treatment if eligibility requirements are met. This can be useful for a mature service business, but the current equity design should not be forced into an S-corporation prematurely.

An S corporation must have only one class of stock for federal tax purposes; voting differences are generally allowed, but distribution and liquidation rights need to remain identical. That can conflict with a bespoke economic waterfall or true profits-interest structure.

If an S election is considered later, the tax advisor should review:

- all outstanding equity and phantom instruments;
- distribution/liquidation rights;
- shareholder eligibility;
- reasonable compensation/payroll for shareholder-employees;
- state tax effects;
- whether incentive arrangements could be treated as a second class of stock.

### Option C — C corporation

A C corporation becomes more compelling if the company shifts toward:

- institutional venture financing;
- a broad employee stock-option program;
- major external equity rounds;
- venture-style preferred stock;
- a product/IP company whose financing strategy is more important than partnership-tax flexibility.

That is not the current business plan.

## Illinois filing and entity maintenance

Current Illinois Secretary of State filing fees include:

- Articles of Organization: $150;
- foreign LLC admission: $150;
- LLC annual report: $75.

The legal budget should also include registered-agent cost (if outsourced), attorney drafting/review, tax setup, accounting, insurance, local licensing, and any foreign qualification.

## Delaware analysis

### Why not Delaware by default

A Delaware entity with real operations in Illinois would generally still need to qualify to transact business in Illinois. This can produce:

- Delaware registered agent;
- Delaware annual tax;
- Delaware internal-affairs law;
- Illinois foreign qualification;
- Illinois registered agent;
- Illinois annual filing obligations;
- Illinois/Chicago tax and licensing obligations anyway.

As of 2026, Delaware's annual tax on a domestic LLC is $400.

For the current owner-operated technology-services model, that is mostly additional machinery without an identified offsetting benefit.

### When Delaware becomes rational

Revisit Delaware when there is a concrete reason:

- institutional investor requires it;
- planned venture financing;
- conversion to a venture-standard Delaware C corporation is imminent;
- sophisticated M&A or investment transaction makes Delaware internal-affairs law materially valuable.

Do not choose Delaware because “startups are in Delaware.” Entity choice should follow the capital and governance model.

## Michigan analysis

### Foreign qualification

Michigan states that a foreign LLC that is “transacting business” in Michigan must obtain a Certificate of Authority. The phrase is technical and depends on actual activities and statutory exceptions.

The founder roster alone should not determine qualification. Instead, create an operational trigger review when any of these become true:

- the company has an employee regularly working from Michigan;
- a home or office is being held out as a company location;
- a founder/agent routinely executes company business from Michigan;
- the company develops recurring Michigan customers or on-site delivery;
- company property is maintained there;
- local permits/accounts are opened;
- payroll, unemployment, or workers-compensation obligations arise.

Counsel should then determine whether formal foreign qualification is required.

### Payroll / withholding

Michigan Treasury states that an out-of-state business with Michigan employees can have Michigan withholding obligations when Michigan nexus/physical presence is created. Michigan also has special guidance for remote Michigan workers and reciprocity with Illinois.

The company should not improvise payroll across state lines. Before the first Michigan employee payroll:

1. determine employee vs. contractor correctly;
2. determine Michigan nexus;
3. register withholding/unemployment accounts as required;
4. confirm reciprocity handling based on worker residence and work location;
5. confirm workers-compensation coverage;
6. confirm whether foreign qualification is required.

### Workers compensation

Michigan's workers-compensation rules can apply at relatively low employee counts, including one employee working at least 35 hours per week for 13 weeks during the prior 52 weeks or three employees at one time. Actual coverage should be confirmed with the broker based on the final employment structure.

## Illinois and multi-state pass-through taxation

If the LLC is taxed as a partnership, Illinois generally follows the federal partnership classification. Illinois currently imposes a 1.5% replacement tax on partnerships and S corporations, and Illinois has an elective pass-through entity tax regime. Nonresident owners can trigger withholding/payment and filing issues.

A CPA should model at least:

- owner residence by state;
- expected company net income;
- guaranteed payments or other compensation;
- Illinois replacement tax;
- Illinois PTE election;
- Michigan resident-owner treatment;
- Illinois/Michigan credits or reciprocity where applicable;
- local taxes;
- self-employment tax;
- tax-distribution policy.

This model should be completed before final equity percentages are signed because after-tax economics can differ materially from the headline ownership percentages.

## CTA / BOI

As of 2026-08-19, FinCEN states that entities created in the United States and their beneficial owners are exempt from Corporate Transparency Act beneficial-ownership-information reporting under the 2025 interim final rule.

Action: **do not create a mandatory domestic BOI filing task from stale startup checklists.** Re-check FinCEN on the actual formation date because the rule has changed significantly in recent years.

## Chicago business license

Chicago's municipal code states that businesses must be licensed to operate in the City unless another specific license, regulated license, preemption, or permit applies. Each location requiring a license is separately licensed.

Before launch, identify:

- the principal business location;
- whether it is a commercial office, coworking location, or residence;
- which license category BACP assigns to the activity;
- zoning/home-occupation implications if the company is actually operated from a residence;
- whether remote administrative work is distinguishable from making the residence the company business location.

Do not assume that forming an Illinois LLC substitutes for Chicago licensing.

## Chicago and Illinois cloud/software tax

This is one of the highest-priority tax issues for the proposed Microsoft/distributor model.

### Chicago

Chicago's Personal Property Lease Transaction Tax includes certain software licenses and “nonpossessory computer leases.” The current ordinance sets the tax at 15% and includes sourcing rules based in part on the device/location from which a customer accesses the computer service. Mandatory ancillary services can be included in the taxable lease price even if separately stated, while truly optional services may be treated separately.

### Illinois

Illinois changed its lease/rental tax treatment effective 2025. IDOR states that most software licenses can avoid lease tax if specified license conditions are satisfied and that property subject to a qualifying local lease transaction tax such as Chicago's is exempt from the state lease-receipts tax under the new regime.

### Why this matters to the business model

If `[COMPANY_NAME]` becomes the seller/reseller/lessor of cloud subscriptions and also packages governance or support, contract wording and billing architecture can affect tax treatment.

Before reseller launch, obtain a written tax memo addressing:

- distributor versus reseller contracting roles;
- who collects tax;
- customer-location sourcing;
- Chicago PPTLT registration and collection;
- resale/re-lease documentation;
- Illinois software-license requirements;
- optional versus mandatory support/services;
- invoice line-item architecture;
- price changes and pass-through taxes;
- exemptions and exemption documentation.
