# Partner Strategy and Evaluation

## Source and status

- Status: draft working strategy for launch planning
- Purpose: define the partner model, the decision criteria, and the launch sequencing for partnerships and distribution
- Scope: partner categories, distributor and CSP evaluation, specialist partnerships, and governance requirements
- Not legal or channel advice: requires verification against current Microsoft partner rules, licensing policies, and counsel review

## Strategic principle

The company should remain the customer’s strategic and accountable technology partner while using external organizations for distribution, specialist capability, regulated work, and operational coverage. The company’s value is in advising, integrating, governing, and servicing the customer environment—not in acting as a commodity license reseller or a generic help desk.

## Core partner model

### 1. Microsoft partner path

Purpose:

- credibility
- technical enablement
- co-selling and referral opportunities
- marketplace visibility
- Microsoft incentives and partner program benefits
- licensing administration and support where appropriate

The company should pursue a Microsoft partner model, but the exact designation should be selected only after program requirements are reviewed.

### 2. Distributor / CSP model

The company may use an authorized cloud distributor or indirect CSP to simplify computing, billing, and provisioning operations for certain licensing and cloud subscriptions.

This is useful when:

- the company wants to reduce internal billing complexity;
- the customer needs licenses and admin support;
- the company wants a consistent provisioning and renewal path;
- the company wants to preserve focus on services and governance, not reseller administration.

This model should be supplemental to the core service model and not the company’s primary value engine.

### 3. Specialist partner model

The company should use specialists for areas where independence, regulated work, or deep capability is required.

Examples:

- cybersecurity specialists
- compliance and audit support
- legal and tax advisors
- accounting and financial controls partners
- telecom and networking
- field services and hardware integration
- development and integration specialists
- data and analytics specialists

### 4. Referral and channel relationships

The company should also maintain referral relationships with:

- accountants
- lawyers
- insurance brokers
- consultants
- software vendors
- telecom providers
- MSPs and specialists

These are not the same as distribution or channel programs; they are relationship and lead-generation channels that require governance and clear referral rules.

## Decision framework

### A. What we should do internally

These are core responsibilities that should remain with the company at launch:

- customer strategy and discovery
- target-state architecture
- project ownership and accountability
- implementation oversight
- service design and standards
- governance design and operational handoff
- customer communication and escalation
- documentation and standards
- commercial ownership and scope control

### B. What can be partner-led or outsourced

These functions may be delegated or supported by partners:

- licensing provisioning and billing
- some Microsoft / cloud subscription management
- specialist security assessments
- regulated audit or attestation work
- telecom services or physical installation
- field services and hardware deployment
- specialized software engineering or custom development where required

### C. What should be handled through referral only

These should generally remain referral or advisory relationships unless a specific customer need justifies deeper integration:

- legal counsel
- tax counsel
- accounting and financial review
- insurance and risk coverage
- specialist consulting leads

## Required launch partner categories

The following categories should be evaluated as part of launch planning:

1. Microsoft partner / distributor pathway
2. Microsoft indirect CSP or cloud distribution vendor
3. Security specialist partner
4. Compliance / audit support partner
5. Legal advisor
6. Tax and accounting advisor
7. Insurance broker
8. Networking / telecom partner
9. Hardware procurement / field services partner
10. Development or integration specialist partner

## Distributor / CSP evaluation criteria

When evaluating a software distribution or cloud partner, compare these factors:

- supported products and licensing scope
- Microsoft partner status and route to market
- billable support model
- provisioning and renewal workflows
- consolidated billing and tax treatment
- support and escalation ownership
- economics and margin structure
- customer onboarding process
- API or automation integration capability
- security and data-handling requirements
- customer transition or termination process
- account management and enablement quality
- contract complexity and legal friction

The decision should not be made solely on margin. Serviceability, risk reduction, and customer experience matter more than raw reseller economics.

## Pax8 as a candidate

Pax8 is a reasonable candidate to evaluate as part of the distributor or indirect CSP strategy. It may be attractive because it supports cloud software and partner workflows, but it should be treated as one option in a structured evaluation, not as the default assumption.

The company should compare it against:

- current partner program requirements;
- pricing and margin impact;
- customer support model;
- licensing administration complexity;
- available enablement and training;
- support for the company’s service model.

## Partner qualification requirements

Every material partner relationship should define:

- role and scope
- qualification criteria
- customer ownership model
- contracting party
- pricing and referral economics
- confidentiality and data handling
- security requirements
- insurance requirements
- liability and indemnification
- support and escalation path
- service quality reviews
- conflict and independence requirements
- termination and transition rules
- review cadence

## Partner governance rules

1. The company owns the customer relationship.
2. The partner does not replace company accountability.
3. Scope, ownership, and escalation must be documented before the relationship is used on client work.
4. Any specialist work must be reviewed for independence, competence, and conflict.
5. Customer data handling and security requirements must be reviewed before onboarding.
6. Exit and transition planning should be defined before first use.
7. Periodic review should be performed based on quality, risk, and contract clarity.

## Recommended launch sequence

### Phase 1 — internal foundation

- confirm the company service model;
- confirm the customer ownership model;
- define internal-only responsibilities;
- identify which capabilities are not internal at launch;
- document likely partner categories.

### Phase 2 — partner shortlist

- identify at least two candidate partners for critical categories;
- document required capability and evaluation criteria;
- review partner security posture and contractual risk;
- assess pricing, support, and transition requirements.

### Phase 3 — distributor and CSP route selection

- evaluate Microsoft partner route and licensing administration model;
- compare distributor or indirect CSP candidates such as Pax8 and others;
- define the operational model for license provisioning, billing, and renewal;
- confirm customer support and customer ownership responsibilities.

### Phase 4 — pilot partner relationships

- start with a narrow set of pilot relationships;
- document responsibilities and escalation rules;
- validate the operating process with real customer work;
- review whether the partner is strengthening the business model or creating new friction.

### Phase 5 — formal governance and review

- review the partner relationship for concentration risk;
- evaluate service quality and customer outcomes;
- confirm whether the relationship remains strategic or should be diversified.

## Recommended decision statement

The company should pursue a practical partner ecosystem that supports professional services, licensing administration, governance, and specialist delivery without becoming dependent on any single vendor or relationship. The company remains the strategic accountable entity for the customer, while trusted partners support operational and specialist needs.

## Open items to resolve

- which Microsoft pathway is appropriate under current rules;
- whether the company should pursue a distributor or CSP route immediately or later;
- whether licensing resale is a launch capability or a future capability;
- which exact partner candidates are approved for evaluation;
- which partner categories are essential before the first pilot customer;
- which specialists should be retained as referral-only versus active delivery partners.

## Practical next step

Build a single partner scorecard and shortlist with at least the following fields:

- category
- candidate name
- purpose
- capability fit
- contract complexity
- margin/risk profile
- support model
- customer ownership considerations
- status: evaluate / shortlist / approved / rejected

This will allow the company to move from broad strategic intent to operational decision-making without overcommitting too early.
