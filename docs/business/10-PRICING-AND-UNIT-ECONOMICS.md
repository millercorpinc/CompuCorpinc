# Pricing and Unit Economics

## Purpose

Provide the commercial framework for pricing, validating, and operating the service portfolio while distinguishing current working models from older, incomplete estimates.

## Pricing principles

- Price the outcome, risk, complexity, and continuing obligation—not merely hours.
- Use hours as an internal cost and capacity model even when the customer price is fixed.
- Separate discovery from implementation when uncertainty is material.
- Use fixed fees only when prerequisites, assumptions, and exclusions are clear.
- Protect margin through standardization, change control, and customer responsibilities.
- Include sales, project management, documentation, quality review, rework, warranty, partner, tooling, and support burden.
- Recurring pricing must reflect actual operational responsibility and risk.
- Licensing should be transparent and commercially separate from services.
- Discounting requires a business reason and explicit authority.
- Historical estimates that predate detailed work decomposition must not override later, more complete models.

## Commercial architecture

The Microsoft-oriented model has three distinct layers:

1. One-time project or remediation fee
2. Microsoft and related licenses passed through through the approved indirect-CSP/distributor pathway
3. Ongoing Microsoft Security and Governance or broader managed service

This structure prevents licensing economics from obscuring service value and support responsibility.

## 1. Paid assessment

Best for inherited, complex, uncertain, or poorly documented environments.

Typical scope:

- Business and stakeholder discovery
- Current-state inventory
- Identity, device, security, collaboration, data, and administration review
- Provider and ownership review
- Risk and gap analysis
- Target-state recommendation
- Prioritized roadmap
- Implementation options and estimates

Assessment price should reflect environment size, access quality, systems, locations, stakeholder count, and evidence depth.

The assessment should be independently useful even when the customer does not purchase implementation.

## 2. Secure Microsoft 365 Foundation

### Foundation Core

Primary intended segment:

- Fewer than 10 users
- Controlled environment
- Essential identity, device, security, collaboration, incident-readiness, and documentation baseline

Working model:

- Estimated delivery effort: 26–32 hours
- Working price range: $6,000–$9,000
- Historical sizing guidance:
  - 1–3 users: approximately $6,000
  - 4–7 users: approximately $7,000–$8,000
  - 8–10 users: approximately $9,000

### Full Foundation

Primary intended segment:

- Generally 10 or more users
- Customer requiring the complete operating and security baseline

Working model:

- Base controlled-environment estimate: 46 hours
- Working price range: $12,000–$18,000
- Higher pricing or separate assessment for provider-transition risk, migration, hybrid identity, unsupported devices, complex data, regulated requirements, multiple entities or locations, incomplete records, or incident conditions

### Historical estimate status

An earlier $3,500–$7,500 Foundation range was created before the service was decomposed into the 46-hour delivery model. It is historical and superseded for Full Foundation planning.

It may not be used as the current Full Foundation baseline without an explicit redesign of scope and delivery effort.

## 3. Time and materials

Use when:

- Requirements cannot be reliably bounded
- The customer controls key dependencies
- Legacy conditions are unknown
- The work is investigative or iterative
- The customer requests flexible augmentation rather than an outcome-based package

Time-and-materials work still requires objectives, priorities, reporting, access controls, and an agreed budget or not-to-exceed mechanism.

## 4. Fractional advisory retainer

Use for:

- Technology strategy
- Roadmap and budget
- Executive decision support
- Vendor and platform governance
- Security and risk prioritization
- Policy and governance
- Solution selection
- Customer and partner coordination

Historical working range:

- Approximately $2,000–$10,000 monthly depending on access, cadence, scope, executive involvement, and responsibility

Retainers should define:

- Included cadence and hours or capacity
- Decision and escalation access
- Deliverables
- Out-of-scope project work
- Travel and partner costs
- Carryover rules
- Response expectations

## 5. Microsoft Security and Governance subscription

Use after the customer has a supportable baseline.

Potential recurring scope:

- Identity and privileged-access review
- Device and endpoint review
- Secure configuration maintenance
- Microsoft platform change management
- User and device administration
- License oversight
- Documentation maintenance
- Risk and exception review
- Roadmap and quarterly review
- Automation monitoring
- Vendor coordination
- Selective escalation

Historical working ranges:

- Approximately $500–$2,000+ monthly
- Approximately $5–$15 per user monthly for lighter governance

These ranges require a defined minimum fee because very small customers still create fixed review, administration, documentation, and relationship costs.

## 6. Comprehensive managed bundles

Historical hypotheses:

- Core: approximately $75–$100 per user monthly
- Pro: approximately $125–$150 per user monthly
- Enterprise: approximately $175+ per user monthly

Do not launch broad managed bundles until the company approves:

- Help-desk boundary
- Service hours
- Response and resolution targets
- Monitoring
- Tooling
- Staffing and coverage
- Included projects and changes
- Onsite support
- After-hours support
- Escalation and partner model
- Minimum customer standard
- Gross-margin model

## 7. Licensing model

Working commercial principle:

- Use the approved indirect-CSP/distributor pathway
- Present licenses clearly, generally at Microsoft list price unless an approved model states otherwise
- Reconcile quantity, term, renewal, price change, and customer authorization
- Do not depend on license markup as the primary profit engine
- Separate license-price changes from service-price changes
- Define who owns provisioning, billing, support escalation, cancellation, and transition

Pax8 is the presumptive launch distributor, subject to current diligence and contracting.

## 8. Required cost model

For each service calculate:

### Direct delivery

- Principal labor
- Employee labor
- Contractor or subcontractor labor
- Partner delivery cost
- Travel and onsite cost
- Software, cloud, and temporary project tooling

### Indirect delivery

- Discovery and presales
- Solution design
- Project management
- Customer communication
- Documentation
- Quality and security review
- Testing and acceptance
- Closeout and lessons learned

### Risk and lifecycle

- Expected rework
- Warranty burden
- Included support
- Incident or rollback contingency
- Customer delay
- Bad-debt allowance
- Payment fees
- Insurance allocation
- Administrative overhead
- Management overhead

## 9. Capacity model

Track:

- Total available hours
- Billable utilization
- Presales load
- Administrative load
- Service-management load
- Recurring-service committed capacity
- Project overlap
- Partner dependencies
- Specialist constraints
- Principal bottlenecks
- Vacation and absence coverage
- Customer concentration

Fixed-fee work is economically safe only when the company compares actual effort with the service baseline.

## 10. Gross-margin discipline

Each service must define:

- Target gross margin
- Minimum acceptable margin
- Base effort
- Complexity factors
- Expected delivery variance
- Contingency
- Maximum included warranty or support
- Conditions requiring a change order
- Discount authority

Until a formal target is approved, every proposal should at least model:

- Expected case
- Conservative case
- Stress case

Historical long-run operating profit hypothesis: approximately 20–25%. This is not a service gross-margin target and must not be confused with one.

## 11. Payment and cash-flow policy topics

The approved commercial policy should define:

- Deposit or commencement payment
- Milestone billing
- Recurring billing timing
- Payment terms
- Expenses and pass-through costs
- Tax treatment
- Late payment
- Suspension rights
- Cancellation
- Nonrefundable license commitments
- Change orders
- Refund and warranty boundaries

Project structures should avoid financing the customer’s work from company cash.

## 12. Complexity factors

Foundation and other packaged work should be adjusted for:

- User and device count
- Number of tenants, domains, entities, and locations
- Provider cooperation
- Access quality
- Hybrid identity
- Migration
- Data volume and cleanup
- Unsupported devices or applications
- Regulatory requirements
- Existing compromise or incident
- Advanced retention or legal requirements
- Network and virtual-workspace dependencies
- Custom integrations
- Customer decision speed
- Documentation quality

## 13. Pricing validation program

Validate through:

- Buyer interviews
- Paid pilots
- Competitive research
- Actual delivery-time evidence
- Win/loss analysis
- Margin review
- Support and warranty tracking
- Project-to-recurring conversion
- Renewal and expansion behavior

For each pilot capture:

- Original estimate
- Actual hours by work area
- Customer delay
- Rework
- Scope change
- Partner cost
- Warranty and support effort
- Customer outcome
- Price objection or acceptance
- Recommended model change

## 14. Historical launch financial hypotheses

Recovered early-stage planning assumptions:

- Initial setup cost: approximately $5,000–$10,000
- Monthly operating expense: approximately $1,500–$3,000 before meaningful staffing expansion
- Average recurring client revenue: approximately $3,000–$5,000 monthly
- Break-even hypothesis: approximately three recurring clients
- Initial planning horizon: 18 months

These are not validated forecasts. The financial model must be rebuilt using current product costs, insurance, legal, tax, labor, sales cycle, collection timing, and actual role capacity.
