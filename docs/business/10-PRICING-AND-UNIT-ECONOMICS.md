# Pricing and Unit Economics

## Purpose

Provide a commercial framework for validating and operating the service portfolio without treating historical price ranges as final.

## Pricing principles

- Price the outcome, risk, complexity, and continuing obligation—not merely hours.
- Use fixed fees only when assumptions, prerequisites, and exclusions are clear.
- Separate discovery from implementation when environment uncertainty is high.
- Protect margin through standardization, change control, and customer responsibilities.
- Include partner, licensing, support, documentation, rework, and management costs.
- Recurring pricing must reflect actual support burden and risk ownership.
- Discounting requires a business reason and explicit approval.

## Commercial models

### Paid assessment

Best for uncertain or inherited environments.

Includes:

- Discovery
- Current-state review
- Risk and gap analysis
- Target-state recommendation
- Prioritized roadmap
- Implementation estimate

### Fixed-fee foundation project

Best for a defined customer profile and controlled prerequisites.

Historical hypothesis for a secure workplace foundation:

- Approximately $3,500–$7,500 for a straightforward implementation
- Higher pricing for takeover risk, migrations, complex identity, nonstandard endpoints, data cleanup, regulated environments, or multi-site conditions

### Time and materials

Use when requirements or dependencies cannot be reliably bounded.

### Advisory retainer

Use for fractional leadership, roadmap, governance, vendor coordination, and executive decision support.

Historical hypothesis:

- Approximately $2,000–$10,000 monthly depending on scope and access

### Governance subscription

Use for recurring configuration, security, documentation, change, and roadmap oversight.

Historical hypotheses:

- Approximately $500–$2,000+ monthly
- Approximately $5–$15 per user for lighter governance

### Comprehensive managed bundle

Use only after support boundaries, service hours, tooling, staffing, and escalation are defined.

Historical hypotheses:

- Core: approximately $75–$100 per user
- Pro: approximately $125–$150 per user
- Enterprise: approximately $175+ per user

## Required cost model

For each service calculate:

- Direct founder labor
- Contributor or subcontractor labor
- Software and tooling
- Licensing and distributor costs
- Partner costs
- Sales and presales effort
- Project management
- Documentation and quality review
- Expected rework
- Support and warranty burden
- Administrative overhead
- Payment fees and bad-debt allowance
- Risk contingency

## Required capacity model

Track:

- Available delivery hours
- Billable utilization
- Presales and administrative load
- Recurring-service load
- Project overlap
- Specialist constraints
- Founder bottlenecks
- Customer concentration

## Gross-margin discipline

Each service should define:

- Target gross margin
- Minimum acceptable margin
- Expected delivery variance
- Conditions that require repricing or change order
- Maximum included support or warranty

## Pricing validation

Validate through:

- Buyer interviews
- Paid pilots
- Competitive research
- Delivery-time evidence
- Win/loss analysis
- Margin review
- Customer expansion and renewal behavior

Do not publish detailed pricing until the underlying scope and economics are tested.
