# Market and Positioning

## Initial ideal customer hypothesis

Organizations with approximately 10–150 employees that use Microsoft 365 and have outgrown informal technology operations.

Broader practical range: approximately 1–200 users.

## Common conditions

- No experienced internal technology executive
- One IT generalist or a legacy outsourced provider
- Inconsistent identity, device, collaboration, and data controls
- Manual workflows and disconnected systems
- Weak documentation and unclear ownership
- Increasing customer, insurer, or compliance requirements
- Need for modernization without a large transformation program

## Buyer roles

- Owner / founder
- President
- COO
- CFO
- IT manager
- Operations leader
- Security or compliance leader

## Positioning

Working category:

**Advisory-led technology and managed-governance partner**

Working value proposition:

> We design secure, governable technology environments that owners can actually run.

Working delivery promise:

> Enterprise precision, small-team speed.

These phrases require testing and are not approved taglines.

## Differentiation

- Senior founder involvement
- Strategy before tooling
- Security and documentation built into delivery
- Microsoft depth without Microsoft-only dogma
- Cross-system automation and integration
- Repeatable architecture and service standards
- Partner ecosystem for specialized outcomes
- Recurring ownership after implementation

## Validation needs

- Interview at least 10 target buyers
- Test urgency, language, budget, and objections
- Identify trigger events
- Compare assessment-first and implementation-first sales motions
- Determine whether a vertical focus improves acquisition
