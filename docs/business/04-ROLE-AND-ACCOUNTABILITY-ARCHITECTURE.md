# Role and Accountability Architecture

## Purpose

Define the operating functions the company requires without assigning them to historical individuals.

This document supersedes all prior named-person role assignments. Actual participants, owners, employees, contractors, titles, compensation, authority, and reporting relationships remain future decisions.

## Design principles

- Roles exist because the business requires accountable outcomes, not because a specific person was previously associated with them.
- One individual may perform multiple roles during launch.
- No role may be left implicitly owned.
- Authority must match accountability.
- Material legal, financial, security, pricing, and customer commitments require documented decision rights.
- Separation of duties should increase as revenue, staffing, and risk increase.
- Contractors and partners may perform work, but company accountability must remain explicit.

## Required executive functions

Accountable for:

- Market development
- Target-account strategy
- Prospecting and relationship development
- Discovery coordination
- Opportunity qualification
- Account leadership
- Commercial feedback and market intelligence
- Proposal progression
- Customer expansion and renewal
- Referral and channel development

Typical potential titles:

- President
- Chief executive officer
- Chief revenue officer
- Head of Sales and Partnerships
- Managing principal

### 2. Technology, security, and strategy leadership

Accountable for:

- Business and technology strategy
- Service and product architecture
- Microsoft alignment
- Security architecture
- Technical standards and exceptions
- Customer target-state design
- Solution ownership
- Architecture and high-risk change review
- Technical quality
- Partner and distributor technical relationships
- New-offering development
- Reusable patterns and intellectual property

Typical potential titles:

- Chief technology officer
- Chief technology and information officer
- Chief strategy and technology officer
- Technology principal
- Security and architecture principal

### 3. Delivery and service operations

Accountable for:

- Delivery planning and execution
- Staffing and contributor coordination
- Project and service operations
- Capacity planning
- Change control
- Escalation coordination
- Documentation completion
- Quality assurance
- Delivery economics
- Customer handoff
- Converting designed services into repeatable operations

Typical potential titles:

- Chief operating officer
- Head of Delivery
- Service delivery director
- Operations principal
- Professional services leader

### 4. Finance, legal, risk, and administration

Accountable for:

- Banking and cash controls
- Accounting and financial reporting
- Forecasting and reserves

- Corporate records
- Vendor and contractor administration
- Risk register and compliance administration

Typical potential titles:

This function may be combined with commercial or technology leadership at launch.

## Delivery roles

### Account lead

- Owns customer relationship and decisions
- Maintains business context and roadmap
- Coordinates proposals, renewals, and expansion
- Ensures commercial and delivery expectations remain aligned

### Solution architect

- Converts discovery into target architecture
- Selects standard patterns and documents exceptions
- Defines dependencies, risks, acceptance, and operational ownership
- Reviews security and maintainability

### Project or delivery lead

- Owns plan, dependencies, status, risks, changes, and acceptance
- Coordinates company, customer, and partner work
- Ensures evidence and documentation are complete

### Technical implementer

- Performs approved technical changes
- Captures execution evidence
- Follows access, testing, rollback, and documentation standards
- Escalates deviations rather than silently improvising

### Security reviewer

- Reviews access, data handling, controls, exceptions, and evidence
- May be the technology leader on lower-risk engagements
- Requires independent review for material conflicts or regulated work

### Service owner

- Owns the definition, economics, quality, metrics, revision history, and lifecycle of a packaged service

### Customer success / governance lead

- Maintains recurring cadence
- Tracks roadmap, risks, decisions, documentation, and service health
- Coordinates platform changes, renewals, and expansion opportunities

## Decision-right categories

### Function-level decisions

May be made by the accountable function within approved budget, policy, service, contract, and risk boundaries.

Examples:

- Standard delivery scheduling
- Approved technical patterns
- Routine customer communications
- Approved partner use
- Standard proposal configuration

### Cross-functional operating decisions

Require the affected functions to agree and record the result.

Examples:

- Nonstandard scope
- Margin exception
- High-risk technical exception
- Material subcontracting
- Customer data-handling deviation
- Recurring-service customization

### Material company decisions

Require the authority defined in the future governance agreement.

Examples:

- Ownership or equity
- Legal or tax structure
- Borrowing or major capital commitment
- Company name
- Major hiring
- New service launch or retirement
- Public pricing-model change
- Major distributor or partner commitment
- Security exception with material company risk
- Entry into a new geography or regulated market
- Sale, merger, dissolution, or transfer of intellectual property

## Launch assignment matrix

Before commercial launch, the company must document who is accountable, responsible, consulted, and informed for at least:

- Lead generation and qualification
- Proposal approval
- Pricing and discounts
- Contract signature
- Architecture approval
- Customer administrative access
- Security exceptions
- Project management
- Technical execution
- Quality assurance
- Customer acceptance
- Invoicing and collections
- Incident response
- Partner engagement
- Service changes
- Repository and documentation ownership

## Capacity model

During launch, a single principal may combine commercial, strategy, architecture, and delivery responsibilities. This is acceptable only when:

- Capacity is measured
- Customer commitments remain realistic
- High-risk work receives a second review where possible
- Documentation is maintained
- Sales does not outrun delivery
- Support obligations remain bounded

The company should add capacity based on demonstrated bottlenecks rather than titles alone.

## Contributor model

Initial capacity may include:

- Independent contractors
- Specialist firms
- Fractional finance or legal providers
- Referral and delivery partners
- Temporary project contributors

Every contributor arrangement must define:

- Scope and outcome
- Customer access
- Confidentiality
- Intellectual property
- Data handling and security
- Quality and acceptance
- Commercial terms
- Insurance where appropriate
- Escalation and replacement
- Knowledge transfer
- Offboarding and access removal

## Prohibited assumptions

Do not assume that any person mentioned in historical Startup discussions:

- Is currently involved
- Is an owner or founder
- Holds a title
- Has authority
- Is available for work
- Has accepted compensation or risk
- Owns prior or future intellectual property

Only current executed agreements and approved decisions may establish those facts.
