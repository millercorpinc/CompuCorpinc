# Financial Plan

## Purpose

Translate the recovered business model into a financial-planning framework while clearly separating historical hypotheses from approved forecasts.

This document is not tax, accounting, investment, or legal advice. Current professional review and a live financial model are required before formation, financing, compensation, hiring, or public pricing decisions.

## 1. Financial objective

Build a capital-light, principal-led technology company in which:

- Paid assessments and implementation projects fund customer acquisition and establish standards
- Recurring advisory and governance revenue creates predictability
- Licensing and partner economics supplement rather than define margin
- Standardization and reusable knowledge improve delivery leverage
- Hiring follows demonstrated capacity constraints and recurring revenue
- Cash controls and customer payment terms prevent the company from financing customer work

## 2. Revenue architecture

### Layer 1 — Assessments

Revenue from:

- Technology, security, and governance assessments
- Integration and automation opportunity assessments
- Virtual-workspace assessments
- Compliance-readiness assessments

Financial role:

- Monetize discovery
- Bound implementation risk
- Improve proposal quality
- Filter poor-fit customers

### Layer 2 — Implementation and remediation

Revenue from:

- Foundation Core
- Full Secure Microsoft 365 Foundation
- Identity, endpoint, security, collaboration, and data remediation
- Migrations
- Virtual workspace
- Integration and automation
- Compliance-control remediation

Financial role:

- Produce material near-term revenue
- Establish the customer baseline
- Generate evidence and reusable assets
- Create recurring-service eligibility

### Layer 3 — Recurring advisory and governance

Revenue from:

- Fractional technology leadership
- Microsoft Security and Governance
- Configuration and security review
- Platform change management
- Documentation and roadmap ownership
- Automation monitoring
- Selective administration and support

Financial role:

- Create predictable revenue
- Improve customer retention and expansion
- Fund stable operating capacity

### Layer 4 — Licensing and partner-related revenue

Revenue may include:

- Licensing margin or incentives
- Referral or partner fees
- Marketplace or packaged-solution economics

Financial role:

- Supplement customer and service economics
- Simplify customer procurement
- Support the partner ecosystem

This layer must not become the main value proposition.

## 3. Recovered historical assumptions

The Startup project developed the following early planning hypotheses:

- Initial setup costs: approximately $5,000–$10,000
- Monthly operating expenses: approximately $1,500–$3,000 before meaningful staffing expansion
- Average recurring client revenue: approximately $3,000–$5,000 monthly
- Break-even: approximately three recurring clients
- Long-run operating profit target: approximately 20–25%
- Initial planning horizon: 18 months with deliberate flexibility

These assumptions are useful starting points but are not validated forecasts.

## 4. Current service working models

### Foundation Core

- Primary segment: fewer than 10 users
- Effort baseline: 26–32 hours
- Working price: $6,000–$9,000

### Full Foundation

- Primary segment: generally 10 or more users or complete-baseline requirements
- Effort baseline: 46 hours for a controlled environment
- Working price: $12,000–$18,000 before complexity adjustments

### Recurring governance

Historical ranges:

- $500–$2,000+ monthly
- $5–$15 per user for lighter governance
- Broader managed bundles at $75–$100, $125–$150, and $175+ per user
- Fractional advisory retainers at $2,000–$10,000 monthly

All ranges require cost, capacity, margin, market, and pilot validation.

## 7. Service cost model

For each service, model:

- Direct delivery hours by role
- Presales and discovery hours
- Architecture and review
- Project management
- Documentation and quality assurance
- Customer communications
- Partner and contractor cost
- Project software or cloud cost
- Expected rework
- Warranty or included support
- Administrative allocation
- Bad-debt and payment-fee allowance
- Risk contingency

The 46-hour Full Foundation baseline includes 10 hours for documentation, QA, and customer review and should not be costed as only configuration time.

## 8. Capacity and utilization

The financial model should use realistic available capacity.

Start with total working hours, then subtract:

- Sales and presales
- Administration
- Product and template development
- Partner management
- Financial and legal work
- Training
- Internal security
- Vacation and absence
- Unplanned support
- Recurring-governance obligations

Do not forecast project capacity using 100% billable utilization.

Track principal bottlenecks separately because strategy, architecture, security review, and customer trust may not be immediately delegable.

## 9. Scenario model

Build at least three scenarios.

### Conservative

- Longer sales cycle
- Lower project conversion
- Higher delivery variance
- Slower recurring attach
- Higher professional and insurance costs
- Customer payment delays

### Base

- Moderate project flow
- Evidence-based Foundation pricing
- Meaningful project-to-recurring conversion
- Controlled contractor use
- No premature broad hiring

### Growth

- Strong channel contribution
- Higher recurring conversion
- Reusable solution patterns
- Selective contractor or employee expansion
- Improved utilization and delivery leverage

Each scenario should show monthly:

- Pipeline
- Signed bookings
- Recognized revenue
- Cash receipts
- Direct cost
- Gross profit
- Operating expense
- Operating profit
- Ending cash
- Recurring revenue
- Capacity utilization

## 10. Break-even analysis

The historical “three recurring clients” hypothesis should be tested using:

- Average monthly recurring revenue
- Direct recurring-service labor
- Tools and partner costs
- Fixed monthly operating expense
- Required project and sales capacity
- Taxes and owner compensation
- Customer concentration risk

Break-even should be calculated both:

- Before owner or principal market compensation
- After reasonable owner or principal compensation

A business that covers software but not the actual labor required to operate is not at economic break-even.

## 11. Cash-flow controls

Recommended policy topics for approval:

- Project deposit before scheduling
- Milestone billing for longer engagements
- Recurring services billed in advance
- Licenses billed in advance or aligned to distributor terms
- Customer authorization for noncancelable commitments
- Clear expense and pass-through treatment
- Receivables review at least weekly during launch
- Suspension rights for overdue balances
- Tax reserve
- Minimum cash reserve
- Approval thresholds for spending and commitments

## 12. Revenue recognition and accounting considerations

Obtain accounting advice for:

- Project milestones
- Retainers
- Prepaid recurring services
- License resale or agent-versus-principal treatment
- Contractor cost
- Referral fees
- Nonrefundable vendor commitments
- Sales tax and other indirect tax
- Deferred revenue
- Capitalized versus expensed software or development

## 13. Compensation and hiring gates

No historical individual is assumed involved.

Before compensation or hiring commitments, define:

- Actual role and accountability
- Employment, contractor, owner, or partner status
- Market compensation
- Variable compensation
- Revenue and margin gate
- Cash-reserve gate
- Performance evidence
- Intellectual-property and confidentiality terms
- Departure and transition obligations

Potential hiring or contractor triggers:

- Sustained pipeline beyond principal capacity
- Recurring obligations consuming approved capacity threshold
- Repeated specialist work with positive economics
- Sales bottleneck with proven close rate
- Delivery bottleneck with documented standard work
- Finance or administration risk beyond outsourced support

## 15. Validation plan

Before relying on the model:

1. Update current software, insurance, legal, accounting, and distributor costs.
2. Model role capacity and reasonable compensation.
3. Price at least three Foundation scenarios using the work breakdown.
4. Model assessment pricing.
5. Model recurring governance at minimum, typical, and high-support conditions.
6. Run conservative cash-flow assumptions.
7. Deliver pilots and record actual hours and costs.
8. Update the model monthly during the first year.
