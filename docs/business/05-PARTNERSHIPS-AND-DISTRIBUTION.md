# Partnerships and Distribution

## Document status

This is the canonical working partnership and distribution strategy.

Partnerships are structural components of the business model, not optional future channels. Specific contracts, economics, current program requirements, and qualified providers require diligence and approval.

## Principle

`[COMPANY_NAME]` should remain the customer's strategic architect and accountable technology partner while using qualified external organizations for distribution, specialist delivery, independent review, regulated work, geographic coverage, and supplemental capacity.

The company should not pretend every competency belongs in-house. It should maintain clear customer ownership, scope boundaries, data handling, accountability, escalation, and independence.

## 1. Microsoft ecosystem strategy

The company should pursue the Microsoft partner capabilities that best support:

- Credibility
- Technical enablement
- Customer licensing and administration
- Managed-service capabilities
- Security capabilities
- Incentives
- Co-selling
- Marketplace participation
- Access to technical and commercial resources

The exact program sequence must be researched against current Microsoft rules before execution. Historical program names or requirements must not be assumed current.

## 2. Indirect CSP and distributor model

### Established operating direction

The launch model should use an authorized indirect CSP/distributor rather than building independent systems for:

- Subscription provisioning
- Consolidated billing
- Credit and payment administration
- License management
- Technical escalation
- Partner enablement
- Marketplace access
- Adjacent cloud and security products
- Financing or payment support
- Automation and integration interfaces

### Presumptive launch pathway: Pax8

Pax8 is the presumptive launch distributor pathway recovered from the Startup project.

This is stronger than a generic example but is not an executed contract. Before launch, the company must complete current diligence covering:

- Microsoft indirect-CSP capabilities and requirements
- Commercial terms and margin
- Billing, taxation, payment, credit, and collections mechanics
- Provisioning and customer administration
- Support responsibility and escalation
- Security and data handling
- API, automation, and integration capabilities
- Product catalog and adjacent security offerings
- Contract terms, termination, and customer transition
- Marketplace or services enablement
- Training and partner support
- Portability if the distributor relationship changes

The operating architecture must remain portable enough to move customers or add another distributor without rebuilding the company.

### Licensing commercial principle

Microsoft licenses should generally be presented as a clean, understandable pass-through layer, commonly at Microsoft list price unless an approved commercial model states otherwise.

The company’s primary value and margin should come from advisory, architecture, implementation, security, automation, governance, and managed services—not opaque license markup.

## 4. Independent professional model

Certain work must remain with qualified independent professionals.

Examples:

- SOC examinations or attestations
- Financial statement audits
- Legal advice and legal opinions
- Tax advice and filings
- Insurance placement
- Other regulated, licensed, or credentialed services

The company may perform:

- Readiness assessment
- Technology control design
- Evidence organization
- Remediation
- Documentation
- Ongoing control maintenance
- Coordination with the independent provider

The company must accurately describe its role and avoid implying that its own readiness or remediation work is an independent attestation.

## 5. Referral and channel relationships

Potential channel categories include:

- Accounting firms
- Legal firms
- Insurance brokers
- Investors and private-equity groups
- Business consultants
- Software vendors
- Telecom providers
- MSPs and technical specialists
- Industry associations

The desired model is coordinated delivery rather than simple lead exchange.

Strong channel relationships should define:

- Customer problem and qualification
- Which party leads the relationship
- Handoff and discovery process
- Commercial and referral economics
- Confidentiality
- Customer consent
- Service boundaries
- Follow-up and measurement

## 6. Partner operating models

### Referral partner

Introduces a qualified opportunity but does not materially participate in delivery.

### Channel or co-selling partner

Coordinates market development, discovery, proposal, or customer relationship activity.

### Delivery subcontractor

Performs defined work under the company’s customer agreement or under a coordinated agreement structure.

### Independent professional

Performs work requiring independence, licensure, or credentials under its own responsibility.

### Distributor or platform partner

Provides licensing, provisioning, marketplace, commercial, support, or platform infrastructure.

### Strategic alliance

Coordinates repeatable offerings, go-to-market, intellectual property, or shared delivery capability.

Each relationship must be explicitly classified because customer ownership, liability, contracting, security, and economics differ.

## 7. Partner qualification requirements

Before a partner receives customer access or represents the company, evaluate:

- Legal identity and good standing
- Relevant capabilities
- References and delivery evidence
- Certifications or credentials where material
- Financial and operational stability
- Security program
- Data handling and privacy
- Personnel screening and access controls
- Insurance
- Contracting and liability
- Conflict of interest
- Independence requirements
- Use of subcontractors
- Geographic and time-zone coverage
- Support and escalation
- Documentation and knowledge transfer
- Commercial fit
- Cultural and communication fit

Qualification depth should match the risk of the relationship.

## 8. Required partner agreement topics

Every material relationship should define:

- Role and scope
- Customer ownership
- Contracting party
- Authority to make commitments
- Pricing, referral fees, margin, and payment
- Confidentiality
- Intellectual property
- Customer data handling
- Security requirements
- Access and offboarding
- Insurance
- Liability and indemnification
- Warranties
- Support and escalation
- Quality and acceptance
- Use of subcontractors
- Conflict and independence requirements
- Non-solicitation or customer-protection terms where lawful and appropriate
- Termination
- Customer and data transition
- Periodic review

## 9. Customer engagement rules

- The customer must understand which party is responsible for each outcome.
- No partner receives customer access without approved scope and security requirements.
- The company must not share customer information merely because a referral relationship exists.
- Partner work must produce the evidence and documentation required by the customer engagement.
- Material partner failure must have an escalation and transition plan.
- The company should avoid permanent dependency on a partner where knowledge transfer is feasible.

## 10. Pax8 diligence decision gate

Before treating Pax8 as fully approved, complete and record:

1. Current account and program requirements
2. Commercial and credit review
3. Contract and legal review
4. Security and privacy review
5. Provisioning and billing workflow test
6. Support-escalation test
7. API and integration assessment
8. Customer-transition and termination review
9. Finance and reconciliation procedure
10. Approved decision record

Until that gate is complete, use the status:

> Presumptive launch distributor; diligence and contracting in progress or pending.

Do not revert the status to “Pax8 is merely an example” unless a later decision explicitly rejects it.

## 11. Partner governance cadence

### Per engagement

- Confirm role and scope
- Confirm access and data handling
- Confirm deliverables and evidence
- Track issues and acceptance
- Complete access removal and knowledge transfer

### Quarterly or semiannual review

- Revenue and pipeline contribution
- Delivery quality
- Support performance
- Security issues
- Customer feedback
- Commercial disputes
- Capacity and capability changes
- Qualification currency
- Strategic fit

### Annual review

- Contract and insurance
- Security documentation
- Credentials
- Financial and operating stability
- Continued need and alternatives

## 12. Metrics

Track:

- Partner-sourced pipeline and revenue
- Partner-assisted revenue
- Delivery margin
- Quality and rework
- Escalation frequency
- Customer satisfaction
- Time to resolve partner incidents
- Concentration by partner
- Distributor billing and provisioning accuracy
- License reconciliation issues
- Qualification and review currency
