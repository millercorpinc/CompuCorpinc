# 1. Accounting, CPA, tax, bookkeeping, and advisory firms

## Why this segment stands out

Accounting/tax combines four unusually attractive attributes:

1. valuable financial and taxpayer data;
2. explicit security-plan obligations for tax professionals;
3. many small/owner-controlled practices; and
4. a dense Illinois professional ecosystem.

The Illinois CPA Society reports more than **20,000 accounting and finance professional members**. Its current membership snapshot says roughly **42% are CPAs in public practice**.

Source: Illinois CPA Society: https://www.icpas.org/about

## External security pressure

The IRS states that federal law requires tax professionals to create, implement, and maintain a written information-security plan to protect client data **regardless of firm size**.

The IRS's 2026 guidance describes requirements including:

- designate responsibility for the security program;
- identify and assess risks;
- evaluate safeguards;
- design and implement safeguards;
- regularly monitor and test the program; and
- use service providers that maintain appropriate safeguards.

Sources:

- IRS, *Written Information Security Plans are essential for tax pros* (2026): https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/written-information-security-plans-are-essential-for-tax-pros
- IRS, *Identity theft information for tax professionals*: https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-central/identity-theft-information-for-tax-professionals
- FTC, *Safeguards Rule: What Your Business Needs to Know*: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know

The FTC expressly lists **tax preparation firms** among examples of financial institutions subject to the Safeguards Rule.

## Likely technology stack

- Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace;
- tax preparation software;
- accounting/bookkeeping platforms;
- document portals;
- e-signature;
- payroll systems;
- bank/payment access;
- remote desktop/access;
- scanning/PDF workflow;
- file storage/sharing;
- password/credential tooling;
- endpoint security and backup.

## Buying triggers

- tax season preparation;
- WISP creation/update;
- insurance renewal;
- client security questionnaire;
- new partner/office;
- remote-work change;
- breach/phishing event;
- software migration;
- acquisition/merger;
- retiring owner handing operations to a successor.

## Offer hypothesis

### Small employer

Technology and Security Baseline Assessment + **Tax/Accounting Security Readiness module**.

Potential module outputs:

- technical-control evidence for WISP support;
- identity/MFA/admin review;
- endpoint and remote-access baseline;
- data-flow/application inventory;
- backup/recovery validation;
- service-provider inventory;
- access lifecycle;
- incident/data-theft response technology runbook;
- remediation plan.

The company should not represent that it provides legal certification of Safeguards Rule compliance. The technology work can support a customer and its counsel/compliance advisors.

### Owner-operated/microbusiness

A narrow “Tax Practice Security Baseline” may be one of the best candidates for a productized microbusiness offering because the external obligation exists even for a one-person practice.

## Channel hypothesis

- Illinois CPA Society educational/sponsorship ecosystem;
- local CPA networks;
- tax practice consultants;
- professional liability/cyber insurance brokers;
- accounting-software consultants;
- referral relationships with larger CPA firms that do not want to deliver IT;
- bank and wealth-advisor relationships.

## Risks

- tax/accounting firms can be extremely price-sensitive;
- seasonal availability is poor near filing deadlines;
- some firms already use specialized IT vendors;
- technology provider contracts and data access require strong safeguards.

**Working priority:** Tier 1.

# 2. Law firms

## Market structure

Illinois State Bar Association reporting on ARDC data said that in 2023 there were **49,361 lawyers in private practice** and approximately half were in firms of 10 lawyers or fewer. Solo practitioners represented about **25.1% of private-practice lawyers** and lawyers in 2–10-lawyer firms about **24.2%**.

Source: ISBA, *Changing Rolls*: https://www.isba.org/ibj/2024/07/lawpulse/changingrolls

This is attorney count rather than firm count, but it establishes a substantial small-practice ecosystem.

## Technology decision structure

ABA's 2024 Solo and Small Firm TechReport found:

- **97% of solos** and **90% of 2–9-lawyer firms** report making their own technology decisions;
- only **41% of solos** and **55% of 2–9-lawyer firms** reported budgeting for technology;
- solos commonly rely on consultants or online resources for support;
- small firms frequently rely on consultants;
- solo/small firms have materially fewer security policies/features than larger firms;
- two-factor-authentication adoption among solos was reported at about **54.7%**.

Source: American Bar Association, *2024 Solo and Small Firm TechReport*: https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-solo-and-small-firm-techreport/

ABA's practice-management summary reported average annual technology spend across respondents of about $13,991; solos generally reported below $3,000 of annual practice-management software spend, while 2–9-lawyer firms commonly reported $10,000–$20,000.

Source: https://www.americanbar.org/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2024/2024-practice-management-techreport/

## Strategic implication by size

### Solo

Strong need and buyer access, but budget pressure makes the full Foundation difficult. Best fit is productized security/continuity or a high-revenue-solo exception.

### 2–9 lawyers

Potential sweet spot:

- direct decision access;
- enough technology spend;
- confidentiality and financial exposure;
- consultant use is culturally normal;
- often no internal technology leadership.

### 10–49 lawyers

Higher budgets and complexity; likely very attractive for assessment, modernization, governance, automation, and incumbent-MSP coexistence.

## Likely stack

- Microsoft 365;
- practice/case management;
- document management;
- PDF/document assembly;
- e-signature;
- legal research;
- court e-filing;
- billing/accounting;
- trust accounting;
- client portal;
- remote access;
- e-discovery tools in litigation firms.

## Primary risk narratives

- confidential client information;
- email compromise;
- trust/closing/payment fraud;
- lost/stolen endpoints;
- weak offboarding;
- excessive file sharing;
- vendor access;
- disaster recovery;
- ethical technology competence and client expectations.

## Offer hypothesis

- baseline assessment;
- Microsoft/email identity hardening;
- device baseline;
- document/sharing governance;
- business continuity/backup validation;
- practice-management integration/automation review;
- client-security evidence pack;
- recurring governance.

## Channel hypothesis

- Chicago Bar Association;
- Illinois State Bar Association;
- legal malpractice/cyber insurers;
- law-practice consultants;
- legal software vendors;
- CPA/referral relationships;
- legal recruiters and fractional law-firm administrators.

The CBA explicitly provides law-practice-management and technology education, making it both a market signal and a possible educational channel.

## Competitive issue

Chicago has multiple IT providers positioning specifically around law firms. The company should not compete solely on “we understand legal IT.”

Better differentiation:

- independent assessment before managed-service displacement;
- Microsoft security architecture;
- evidence/documentation;
- workflow/automation improvement;
- partner-level executive narrative; and
- ability to coexist with existing support.

**Working priority:** Tier 1.

# 3. Healthcare practices and outpatient providers

## Market signal

The SBA Chicago profile reports approximately **69,795 healthcare/social-assistance nonemployer establishments** and **21,208 small employer firms** on its 2022 baseline.

This broad NAICS category includes many organizations outside the desired clinical-practice ICP, so detailed NAICS filtering is required.

Chicago Medical Society says it represents **more than 17,000 physicians**.

Source: https://www.cmsdocs.org/membership

## Security/regulatory pressure

HHS publishes Security Rule guidance specifically for **small providers** and provides a Security Risk Assessment tool for small and medium-sized healthcare practices and business associates.

Sources:

- HHS Security Rule guidance: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/index.html
- HHS risk-analysis guidance: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/guidance-risk-analysis/index.html
- HHS cybersecurity/ransomware guidance: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/cybersecurity/index.html

## Likely stack

- EHR/EMR;
- practice management/scheduling;
- patient portal;
- telehealth;
- payment processing;
- Microsoft 365/email;
- scanning/document workflows;
- endpoint/mobile devices;
- clinical devices where applicable;
- cloud backup;
- identity/SSO integrations.

## Buying triggers

- opening a practice/clinic;
- new EHR;
- acquisition/merger;
- insurer/security review;
- HIPAA risk analysis finding;
- ransomware/phishing event;
- telehealth/remote-work expansion;
- new location;
- departure from a hospital/large group into independent practice.

## Offer hypothesis

Healthcare Baseline Assessment module:

- Microsoft/email/device security;
- technology-side HIPAA safeguard evidence;
- EHR/application and vendor map;
- access lifecycle;
- remote/mobile use;
- backup/recovery;
- BAA/vendor inventory support;
- logging/incident readiness;
- remediation roadmap.

The company should clearly distinguish technology implementation/readiness from legal HIPAA advice or formal compliance certification.

## Channel hypothesis

- Chicago Medical Society;
- specialty medical societies;
- physician practice-management consultants;
- medical billing companies;
- malpractice/cyber brokers;
- healthcare attorneys;
- EHR implementation partners;
- healthcare CPAs.

## Risks

- regulated data access increases company risk;
- healthcare vertical vendors can constrain architecture;
- downtime tolerance is low;
- support expectations can become 24x7 quickly;
- scope must exclude clinical device support unless intentionally developed.

**Working priority:** Tier 1, with strict scope/risk discipline.

# 4. Construction, contractors, trades, and field services

## Market scale

The SBA Chicago profile reports approximately:

- **72,743 nonemployer construction establishments**; and
- **22,426 small employer construction firms**.

Chicagoland Associated General Contractors says its general-contractor members range from smaller specialist companies to large national firms and collectively build private/public projects valued at **more than $12 billion annually**, employing more than 10,000 people.

Source: https://chicagolandagc.org/about-us/

Its public directory currently returns roughly 238 active members across contractors, suppliers, professional services, and related firms, illustrating a concrete channel/list source rather than the whole addressable market.

## Why headcount fails here

Contractors commonly leverage subcontractors, independent trades, temporary/project labor, and external design teams. W-2 headcount can dramatically understate:

- project value;
- number of identities touching files;
- external collaboration;
- payment volume;
- mobile-device surface;
- application complexity.

## Likely stack

- Microsoft 365;
- estimating/bidding;
- project management;
- drawings/document control;
- Bluebeam/Autodesk/Procore/Buildertrend or similar tools;
- accounting/job costing;
- time tracking;
- field photos/forms;
- CRM;
- mobile devices;
- vendor/subcontractor portals;
- e-signature;
- payment/ACH workflows.

## Risk narratives

- vendor impersonation/payment diversion;
- compromised superintendent/project-manager inbox;
- shared accounts;
- unmanaged field devices;
- stale subcontractor/vendor access;
- drawing/specification versioning;
- project data loss;
- ransomware during active jobs;
- customer/GC cybersecurity requirements.

## Offer hypothesis

Field Operations Technology & Security Baseline:

- identity/admin baseline;
- device/mobile management;
- secure external collaboration;
- contractor/vendor access lifecycle;
- project/document architecture;
- payment-change verification process;
- backup/recovery;
- application/integration map;
- workflow-automation opportunities;
- recurring access/governance review.

## Best subsegments

- specialty contractors with office + field workforce;
- commercial contractors;
- contractors serving institutional/enterprise customers;
- project-based firms using multiple SaaS tools;
- operators coordinating large subcontractor ecosystems;
- growing contractors moving from owner-managed IT to formal operations.

Avoid assuming every sole-proprietor trade business is a technology prospect.

## Channels

- Chicagoland AGC;
- specialty trade associations;
- construction CPAs;
- surety/insurance brokers;
- construction attorneys;
- construction software implementers;
- banks serving contractors;
- project-management consultants.

**Working priority:** Tier 1/2.

# 5. Real estate, brokerage, property management, and transaction businesses

## Market scale

The SBA profile reports about **86,117 nonemployer real-estate/rental/leasing establishments** and roughly **9,084 small employer firms**.

Chicago Association of REALTORS says it represents **16,000+ members** across residential/commercial brokerage, development, property management, appraisal, auctions, and related specialties.

Source: https://chicagorealtor.com/membership/

Illinois REALTORS reports approximately **50,000 members** statewide.

Source: https://www.illinoisrealtors.org/about/

## Security problem

Real estate is unusually exposed to business-email compromise and transaction impersonation because legitimate workflows involve:

- high-dollar wires;
- changing counterparties;
- attorneys/title/lenders/brokers;
- email-heavy coordination;
- compressed closing timelines.

Illinois REALTORS continues to publish active scam warnings. In May 2026 it warned about fake “buyers” using malicious virtual-meeting links to infect agents' devices.

Source: https://www.illinoisrealtors.org/blog/scam-alert-beware-of-virtual-meetings-with-fraudsters-posing-as-buyers/

## Segmentation warning

A generic individual agent is not automatically a strong customer. Better targets include:

- teams;
- brokerages;
- property managers;
- commercial practices;
- title/closing-adjacent firms where in scope;
- high-producing independent professionals;
- operators with assistants/contractors and large transaction volume.

## Offer hypothesis

“Transaction Security Baseline”:

- email/M365 identity security;
- domain authentication;
- admin controls;
- endpoint baseline;
- credential/password hygiene;
- wire-change verification workflow;
- secure document sharing;
- recovery/incident procedures;
- annual review.

This may be one of the strongest **productized microbusiness** concepts because the risk story is concrete and understandable.

## Channel opportunity

Chicago Association of REALTORS publicly sells advertising and sponsorship access to its 16,000+ audience, providing an unusually direct route to test channel economics.

Source: https://chicagorealtor.com/about-us/advertise-with-the-chicago-association-of-realtors/

**Working priority:** Tier 2, strongly filtered.

# 6. Finance, insurance, lending, and advisory

## Why attractive

- high-value financial/customer data;
- transaction exposure;
- external supervision and contractual requirements;
- strong recurring security/governance need.

The SBA Chicago profile reports approximately **22,690 finance/insurance nonemployer establishments** and **8,371 small employer firms**.

## Regulatory signal

The FTC Safeguards Rule defines “financial institution” more broadly than everyday language. FTC examples include mortgage lenders/brokers, finance companies, account servicers, wire transferors, collection agencies, credit counselors, certain financial advisors, tax preparation firms, and others.

Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftc-safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know

This segment must be subdivided by regulated activity before outreach. “Finance & insurance” is not one compliance regime.

## Offer hypothesis

- security baseline;
- customer-information data-flow map;
- identity/MFA/device controls;
- vendor/service-provider technology evidence;
- incident readiness;
- backup/recovery;
- recurring evidence and access review.

## Risk

Higher regulatory complexity and incumbent-vendor specialization can increase liability. Target only where the company understands the customer's governing requirements and contractually defines its role.

**Working priority:** Tier 2 selective.

# 8. Nonprofits and associations

## Market scale

A 2026 Chicago Social Impact Atlas built from the April 2026 IRS Business Master File identifies roughly **42,000 active 501(c)(3) nonprofits across the 13-county Chicago-Naperville-Elgin area**.

Source/methodology: https://caparipartners.com/atlas

This population is economically heterogeneous, ranging from volunteer organizations to billion-dollar health/education institutions.

BLS reports nonprofit employment is substantial in Illinois; the state had roughly **570,000 nonprofit jobs** in the referenced federal analysis.

Source: https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2025/article/nonprofit-organizations-state-and-regional-employment-trends.htm

## Why selective opportunity exists

Small/midsize nonprofits often combine:

- donor/member/client PII;
- grants and reporting obligations;
- distributed staff/volunteers;
- Microsoft 365 donated/discounted licensing;
- turnover and access-management problems;
- limited internal IT;
- board governance;
- high sensitivity to downtime and fraud;
- constrained operating budgets.

## Best targets

- 5–99 staff;
- recurring institutional funding;
- professionalized leadership/board;
- sensitive client/member data;
- multiple locations or remote staff;
- material Microsoft/cloud footprint;
- compliance/customer/funder security pressure.

## Offer hypothesis

Assessment + governance may fit better than a broad managed-service bundle, especially if the organization already has commodity IT support.

## Risk

Budget pressure and procurement/grant timing can slow sales. Do not confuse the enormous nonprofit count with a uniformly serviceable market.

**Working priority:** Tier 3 selective.

# Cross-vertical conclusion

The common winning condition is not a particular industry. It is the combination of:

> **valuable operations + high dependence on ordinary cloud technology + little senior technology/security leadership + an external or economic reason to care now.**

The strongest verticals simply make those conditions easier to find, explain, and reach through trusted channels.
