- **Purpose:** quantify the real addressable small-business economy around Chicago, identify the portions that fit the company's operating model, and separate measured facts from modeled market scenarios.

This research should be used to improve targeting, offer design, pricing tests, channel strategy, and customer interviews. It does **not** replace primary customer evidence.

## Executive conclusion

The Chicago market is not constrained by lack of target organizations. The more important problem is **segmentation**.

The SBA's 2025 Chicago metro profile reports approximately **1.099 million small businesses** on a common 2022 data basis. About **898,000 were nonemployer establishments** and about **200,600 were small employer firms**. A newer 2023 Census nonemployer count reported by a secondary synthesis is approximately **916,000**, showing that the zero-payroll business population remains enormous.

The research supports a two-market hypothesis:

1. **Core advisory market:** small employer organizations—especially professional, regulated, knowledge-intensive, and field-service firms—that can support a paid assessment, Foundation implementation, and recurring governance.
2. **Productized microbusiness market:** serious owner-operated and very small firms whose technology/security exposure is meaningful but whose economics do not support a miniature enterprise consulting engagement.

Employee count remains useful, but it should no longer be treated as the primary measure of customer quality. Industry, receipts/revenue, data sensitivity, payment/wire exposure, user/device count, Microsoft/cloud dependence, regulatory or customer obligations, technology maturity, and downtime cost can matter more.

- **Hypothesis:** a strategic interpretation requiring customer or commercial validation.
