# ADR-0004: Chicago Market Tracks and Assessment-Led Launch

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-08-18
- **Decision owner:** Founders
- **Approvers:** Founders, pending explicit approval
- **Related documents:** `docs/launch/07-CHICAGO-MARKET-AND-MICROSOFT-ASSESSMENT-BRIEF.md`, `ops/assessment-pricing-workbook.csv`, `docs/business/02-MARKET-AND-POSITIONING.md`

## Context

The current business direction identifies a 10-150 employee Microsoft-centered customer profile but does not yet select a geographic launch focus, vertical test, or precise entry sequence. Founder planning answers proposed Chicago and Chicagoland, four prospect tracks, and a paid assessment before Microsoft implementation.

## Constraints

- This is a launch experiment, not a permanent vertical commitment.
- The company must remain a technology specialist and must not provide legal advice, formal compliance certification, audit, or attestation.
- Microsoft advanced controls depend on customer licensing and risk.
- Migration and takeover work must not be fixed-priced without sufficient assessment evidence.
- Pricing and founder authority remain subject to founder and professional review.

## Options

### Option A: Horizontal SMB launch

Benefits: broad market and simpler messaging. Costs: weaker differentiation and less focused learning. Risks: dispersed outreach and less reusable industry context. Reversibility: high.

### Option B: Single-vertical launch

Benefits: sharper messaging, stronger repeatability, and clearer referrals. Costs: narrower pipeline and risk of choosing the wrong segment before evidence. Risks: dependence on one market. Reversibility: moderate.

### Option C: Four-track Chicago test

Benefits: preserves a common Microsoft assessment core while comparing physicians/clinics, CPA/accounting, construction/field services, and legal or other regulated professional firms. Costs: more disciplined tracking and module design. Risks: insufficient sample size or inconsistent positioning. Reversibility: high after the first 90-day test.

## Decision

Leave blank until founder approval. Proposed direction: choose Option C. Start in Chicago and Chicagoland with approximately five target accounts per track, flexing to actual warm relationships and trigger events. Lead with a paid, fixed-fee Technology and Security Baseline Assessment. Make the Microsoft baseline one week; offer business-systems and vertical-risk modules as optional one-week additions. Require assessment evidence before migration or takeover work.

## Rationale

This preserves the established Microsoft specialization and target-customer profile while creating a practical learning loop. The core assessment is reusable, optional modules prevent overpromising, and four tracks provide enough contrast to test vertical focus without making an irreversible commitment.

## Consequences

Positive:

- Clear Chicago-area launch motion
- Comparable market evidence across four business types
- Paid discovery reduces migration and fixed-scope risk
- Reusable Microsoft assessment and implementation assets
- Explicit professional and support boundaries

Negative or open:

- Pricing cannot be published until the cost workbook has actual inputs
- Four tracks require consistent metrics and messaging discipline
- Ben and Michael's final titles, authority, and detailed work split remain unresolved
- Legal, insurance, contract, and data-protection review remains required

## Validation and review

Review after the first 90 days or after the first three completed assessments, whichever comes later. Compare response, meeting conversion, assessment close rate, payment timing, planned versus actual hours, price acceptance, implementation conversion, recurring-governance conversion, margin, and referral potential.
